# Waypoint Database Deep Dive (Round 2)

Version-Timestamp: 2026-07-30 18:05:00 UTC-4

Scope: the second, deeper round of database research Claudio requested after round 1 (`stack-validation.md`) confirmed Supabase with conditions. The founder's question, quoted: "is it really necessary to use Supabase, or is there a better, more secure, stable, reliable, and compliant solution for that specifically." Security, stability, reliability, and compliance are the explicit ranking criteria, ahead of cost and convenience. This round expands the candidate set to the managed-Postgres specialists the first two rounds skipped, takes the colocation question seriously (the backend already runs on a Hetzner VPS), and evaluates decoupling auth from the database platform. All sources accessed 2026-07-30 unless noted; every claim carries a [verified]/[inferred]/[assumption] tag per `research/00-RESEARCH-PLAYBOOK.md`. Prices exclude VAT unless stated; re-verify at contract time.

**Executive verdict up front: Supabase does not survive this cut as the database home, and the reason is architectural, not a scandal.** Once auth is decoupled and business logic runs through Waypoint's own API layer (both were already round 1 conditions), Supabase's remaining value is a dashboard and auto-generated client APIs that Waypoint deliberately does not use, delivered by a US entity with a noisy incident cadence. The recommendation is a **split architecture**: personal data (accounts, entitlements, consent ledger, privacy-zone geometries) goes to **Aiven for PostgreSQL** (EU-owned, ISO 27001 plus SOC 2 Type II, 99.99% SLA) in an EU region with a strict IP allowlist to the Hetzner VPS; the **geospatial moat workshop** (OSM-derived layers, lighting scores, crossing graphs: heavy, rebuildable, zero personal data) runs as **self-managed Postgres plus PostGIS on the Hetzner private network** next to the pipeline that consumes it, with pgBackRest backups to off-provider object storage. Auth moves into Waypoint's own API layer (Better Auth library, hardened; Ory Kratos as the security-maximal alternative). Runner-up for the personal-data store: OVHcloud managed PostgreSQL, which becomes especially coherent if the compute fallback to OVH is ever executed.

## 1. The frame: what the database actually holds, and what the four pillars demand

What lives server-side (per the locked privacy architecture in `stack-recommendation.md` section 5): account records, consent ledger, subscription entitlements, preference weights, privacy-zone geometries, route metadata (geometry hashes and scores, raw geometries expiring at 90 days), plus the separate moat dataset (per-edge lighting and crossing layers derived from OSM and municipal data). Health metrics never touch the server. [verified against the prior documents]

Two consequences that decide this round:

1. **Location and route data is personal data under GDPR even without health data.** Privacy-zone geometries are home-address proxies (round 1 risk #8, the Strava lesson). The personal-data store must be held to the founder's four pillars at full strength. [verified reasoning from `research/01-market/regulatory-compliance.md`]
2. **The moat dataset is the opposite profile.** It is large (OSM extracts, raster-derived scores), computationally heavy (spatial joins, tile generation), contains zero personal data, and is a rebuildable build artifact (sources plus pipeline code recreate it). Its reliability requirement is "hours to rebuild", not "zero data loss". Forcing one platform to serve both profiles is what round 1 quietly did, and it is why the Supabase answer looked inevitable. [inferred, high confidence]

The sovereignty angle the founder raised, stated precisely: any US-incorporated provider (Supabase, Crunchy, Google, Microsoft, AWS, Timescale, Xata, Ubicloud) is subject to the US CLOUD Act regardless of where the data physically sits, which is the core Schrems II friction for EU personal data; an EU-incorporated provider (Aiven of Finland, Scaleway of France, OVHcloud of France, Hetzner of Germany) gives a clean answer. [verified] (HostStack managed PostgreSQL Europe buyer's guide stating the CLOUD Act / Schrems II distinction and naming Scaleway FR and Aiven FI as EU-incorporated, https://hoststack.dev/blog/managed-postgresql-europe-buyers-guide, accessed 2026-07-30; the legal mechanics themselves are widely established, [verified] at the level of this secondary source, counsel review recommended before contracts)

### 2.1 Supabase (revisited strictly on this round's criteria)

- **Security.** Attestations are real: SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001, AES-256 at rest, TLS in transit (verified in round 1, https://supabase.com/security). The problem is the default posture: Supabase's core model exposes auto-generated REST APIs protected only by Row Level Security, and RLS must be explicitly enabled per table. This model is the industry's misconfiguration hotspot: the Moltbook breach (January 31, 2026) exposed 1.5M API tokens and 35k emails because RLS was never enabled, and CVE-2025-48757 (CVSS 9.3, May 2025) documented the same pattern mass-produced by the Lovable app generator across hundreds of Supabase-backed apps. [verified] (Wiz disclosure, https://www.wiz.io/blog/exposed-moltbook-database-reveals-millions-of-api-keys; Bastion incident analysis, https://bastion.tech/blog/moltbook-security-lessons-ai-agents/; Legba CVE-2025-48757 breakdown, https://www.legba.app/blog/supabase-rls-data-exposure; all accessed 2026-07-30) Fairness note: these are customer misconfigurations of a documented model, not Supabase infrastructure breaches, and Waypoint's round 1 condition 4 (own API layer, no client-side DB access) already avoids the entire pattern. But that is exactly the point: the client-access model is Supabase's main product surface, and Waypoint's security posture requires switching it off. [inferred]
- **Network isolation from a Hetzner VPS.** IP allowlist (network restrictions) on Postgres and the pooler via CLI: workable and free. No private path to Hetzner; PrivateLink exists for AWS VPCs only. Database endpoint remains on the public internet with TLS plus allowlist. [verified] (https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/network-restrictions and https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/ipv4-address, accessed 2026-07-30)
- **Stability.** Round 1 evidence stands: third-party trackers count 47 incidents in 90 days (mostly control plane), including the July 16, 2026 Frankfurt data-plane incident. [verified in round 1] (https://isdown.app/status/supabase, accessed 2026-07-30)
- **Reliability.** Daily backups on Pro, PITR as a paid add-on, read replicas. Fine. [verified in round 1]
- **Compliance.** GDPR DPA available, EU region (Frankfurt), but US-incorporated (CLOUD Act exposure); HIPAA/BAA requires the $599/month Team plan plus a $350/month add-on. [verified in round 1]
- **PostGIS.** Full PostGIS plus pgrouting as supported extensions. [verified in round 1]

### 2.12 Self-managed Postgres plus PostGIS on the Hetzner VPS (modern tooling)

Hetzner offers no managed database product (its "Managed Server" line is a konsoleH web-hosting product, not cloud DBaaS), so self-managed means owning the stack. [verified] (Better Stack Hetzner review, https://betterstack.com/community/guides/web-servers/hetzner-cloud-review/; selfhost.dev analysis stating no managed Postgres as of May 2026, https://selfhost.dev/blog/aws-rds-vs-hetzner-cloud-cost/; both accessed 2026-07-30)

- **Security.** The decisive advantage: the database can be reachable only on the Hetzner private network (layer 3, isolated per project, no public IP on the DB interface at all), with the Hetzner firewall as a second layer. Zero public attack surface, no allowlist to maintain, no exposure to credential-stuffing scanners. Two caveats: Hetzner private-network traffic is isolated but not encrypted by Hetzner (enable TLS on Postgres anyway), and every CVE patch is founder work on founder time. No attestations exist unless you build the evidence trail. [verified as to network isolation and non-encryption] (Hetzner Networks FAQ, https://docs.hetzner.com/networking/networks/faq/, accessed 2026-07-30)
- **Stability.** No SLA (Hetzner Cloud publishes none, round 1 verified). Single-node at MVP means node reboots are downtime.
- **Reliability.** The 2026 toolchain is genuinely good: pgBackRest (full/differential/incremental, parallel compression, encryption, S3/B2 targets, WAL archiving for PITR, built-in verification) is the community gold standard; WAL-G is the lighter alternative; Patroni plus etcd delivers automated failover in under 30 to 45 seconds for a 3-node cluster when HA is justified. The industry-standard production recipe (Patroni, PgBouncer, pgBackRest) is well documented, including complete Hetzner-specific templates. For Waypoint's MVP the right shape is deliberately smaller: one node, Docker or plain systemd, pgBackRest to off-provider object storage (Backblaze B2 or Hetzner object storage in a different location), weekly `pgbackrest verify`, and a rehearsed quarterly restore drill; add Patroni only when there is a contractor to share on-call. Reliability here equals founder discipline, which is precisely its weakness for personal data. [verified as to the tooling capabilities and standard architecture] (InstaDevOps HA guide, https://instadevops.com/blog/postgresql-high-availability-patroni-pgbouncer-guide/; production deployment guide, https://markaicode.com/howto/how-to-deploy-postgresql-production/; Hetzner Patroni/etcd/HAProxy template, https://github.com/dolr-ai/yral-rishi-hetzner-infra-template; all accessed 2026-07-30; the MVP shape recommendation is [inferred])
- **Compliance.** Hetzner is a German company with EU data centers: the only option here with a 100% EU chain from silicon to entity for both compute and data. GDPR DPA available from Hetzner (standard AVV). You carry the processor-evidence burden yourself. [verified as to Hetzner's German incorporation and EU DCs, common knowledge confirmed across cited reviews; DPA mechanics [assumption] A18]
- **PostGIS.** Anything compiles: latest PostGIS, pgrouting, custom builds, no curated-list risk (the PlanetScale lesson from round 1). [verified by nature of self-hosting]
- **Cost.** 10 to 30 EUR/month for a dedicated CX-class VM, or effectively 0 at MVP if it shares the routing VM. [verified instance pricing in round 1]

## 3. The architecture question round 1 underweighted: colocate or manage remotely

The backend (routing engine plus thin API) already runs on a Hetzner VPS. The choice is:

| Dimension | DB on the Hetzner private network (self-managed) | Managed DB over public internet (TLS plus allowlist) |
|---|---|---|
| Attack surface | Zero public exposure; DB has no public IP; scanners never see it | Public endpoint; TLS enforced; allowlist to the VPS's IP; still visible to the internet and dependent on provider edge security |
| Latency | Sub-millisecond to ~1 ms on the private network [verified, Hetzner vSwitch/network docs and practitioner reports, https://wz-it.com/en/blog/proxmox-cluster-networking-hetzner-private-networks-vswitch/, accessed 2026-07-30] | Roughly 5 to 25 ms RTT Hetzner Falkenstein to Frankfurt-region clouds [inferred from geography; irrelevant to route generation, which tolerates hundreds of ms, round 1 verified] |
| Ops burden | Founder owns patching, backups, PITR, upgrades, restore drills | Provider owns all of it 24/7, with an SLA and credits |
| Jurisdiction | 100% EU chain (Hetzner, Germany) | Depends on provider entity (section 2) |
| Failure accountability | You, at 3 a.m. | A staffed on-call team; contractual SLO |
| Cost | ~0 to 30 EUR/month | ~$60 to $250/month at Waypoint's scale |

The resolution is that Waypoint has two datasets with opposite profiles, so it should not pick one side for both:

- **Moat/geospatial workshop: colocate, self-managed.** No personal data, rebuildable from sources, latency-sensitive to the pipeline and the routing graph build, heavy on storage and spatial compute where managed per-GB pricing hurts. Private network only, pgBackRest to off-provider object storage as cheap insurance against rebuild time. The pillars barely bite here: a day of downtime on this DB delays a graph rebuild, nothing more. [inferred design on verified facts]
- **Personal-data store: managed, EU entity, over TLS plus strict allowlist.** Small (thousands to tens of thousands of rows per table at MVP), precious, compliance-heavy, and the dataset where "who applies the CVE patch at 3 a.m." and "is the PITR actually tested" must not depend on one person's discipline. The public endpoint is acceptable when reduced to: TLS 1.2+, single-IP allowlist (the VPS), strong credentials, provider-side dedicated VM, and an attested operator. [inferred design on verified facts]

This split also kills a subtle round 1 problem: it takes the privacy-zone geometries (the most sensitive server-side data) off the same instance that runs bulk OSM imports and experimental pipeline SQL, shrinking both the blast radius and the DPIA surface. [inferred]

## 6. Final verdict

**Winner: the split architecture.**

1. **Personal-data store: Aiven for PostgreSQL, EU region (Frankfurt class), single node Startup tier at MVP, upgrade to Business (synchronous-standby HA, automatic failover, 99.99% SLA) before public launch.** EU-incorporated operator (the cleanest Schrems II answer among DB specialists), ISO 27001 plus SOC 2 Type II, dedicated VM, annual external security evaluation plus Bugcrowd bounty, PITR standard, service-credit-backed SLA. Connect from the Hetzner VPS over TLS with the service firewall allowlisted to the VPS address only. Execute the DPA and read the subprocessor list at signup (the underlying infra cloud choice matters: prefer an EU-operated infrastructure region if offered; open question below).
2. **Moat/geospatial workshop: self-managed Postgres plus PostGIS on the Hetzner private network** (sharing the routing VM at MVP, its own CX-class VM when the pipeline grows), no public IP, pgBackRest to off-provider object storage with encryption, weekly verify, quarterly restore drill. Latest PostGIS and pgrouting, unconstrained by any vendor's curated list.
3. **Auth: decoupled into Waypoint's API layer via Better Auth** (conditions in section 4), with user tables inside the Aiven Postgres. Ory Kratos is the pre-launch alternative if a security review demands longer-audited componentry.

**Runner-up: OVHcloud managed PostgreSQL (Business plan)** for the personal-data store: EU entity, SOC 2 Type 2 plus ISO 27001/27701, PostGIS-forward, cheaper HA than Aiven, and uniquely coherent with the existing OVH compute fallback (shared vRack private networking if compute ever moves there). Its discount versus Aiven prices in the 2021 fire-and-backups history and a thinner DB-specialist reputation; if budget pressure ever forces the choice, OVH Business with verified cross-DC backups plus an off-provider dump is an acceptable answer. Scaleway is the budget EU third.

**What changed versus the prior two rounds, and why:**

- **Round 1 asked "is Supabase good enough?" and answered yes with conditions. Round 2 asked the founder's actual question, "is there something better on security, stability, reliability, and compliance specifically?", and the answer is yes.** The prior rounds never put an EU-incorporated Postgres specialist on the table; Aiven beats Supabase on every one of the four pillars (EU entity vs US entity, 99.99% credit-backed SLA vs no comparable SLA on Pro, dedicated VMs plus bug bounty vs a shared-platform model whose defaults are the industry's misconfiguration hotspot, PITR standard vs add-on).
- **The auth decoupling flips the economics of the bundle.** Round 1's biggest identified risk (Supabase Auth lock-in) was to be contained; round 2 removes it instead, at near-zero cost, because the thin API layer was already in the plan. With auth gone, Supabase's remaining differentiators are features Waypoint's own security conditions disable.
- **The colocation question got a real answer: split the stores.** Round 1 treated "the database" as one thing. Separating the rebuildable geospatial workshop (colocated, self-managed, private-network-only) from the small precious personal-data store (managed, attested, EU) improves every pillar simultaneously and shrinks the DPIA surface.
- **New facts that shifted the board:** Crunchy Data acquired by Snowflake (June 2025), Neon by Databricks (May 2025, round 1): the US specialist consolidation wave makes the independent EU specialist and self-managed paths more attractive on a 5-year horizon. The Moltbook breach and CVE-2025-48757 (January 2026 / May 2025) crystallized why Supabase's default posture and Waypoint's security posture point in opposite directions. Hetzner confirmed as having no managed DB product, and the only managed-ops layers inside its perimeter (Ubicloud, Xata BYOC) are young US vendors without the attestations the personal-data store needs.
- **What did not change:** Postgres plus PostGIS as the data platform (every serious candidate is vanilla Postgres, so this round hardens, not reopens, that decision); Hetzner as compute home; the on-device health-data line as the load-bearing compliance wall; the disqualifications of Firestore and PlanetScale.

**Migration and exit story for the winner (better than Supabase's, and that is the point):**

- Aiven is unforked Postgres: `pg_dump`/`pg_restore` or logical replication out on any day, to any of the candidates in section 2 or to the already-running self-managed tier on Hetzner. The moat DB already proves the self-managed toolchain works, so the exit destination is rehearsed by construction. [inferred on verified vanilla-Postgres facts]
- Auth exits with the database instead of against it: Better Auth's tables are ordinary rows in our own schema, so there is no `auth.users` vendor schema, no JWKS hybrid period, no "auth moves last or never". Round 1's hardest migration problem is deleted rather than mitigated. [inferred]
- Provider-failure insurance is uniform across both stores: nightly off-platform encrypted dumps (personal-data store) and pgBackRest repos (moat store) in object storage held by a third provider, plus infrastructure-as-code for the VPS side. The OVH fire case law is the standing reminder of why the off-provider copy is non-negotiable. [inferred, on the verified OVH rulings]

**Cost honesty (the founder ranked cost below the pillars, but it must be stated):** the split architecture at MVP is roughly $80 to $150/month for both stores combined versus Supabase Pro's $25 plus PITR add-on. At 10k MAU it is roughly $230 to $310 versus roughly $40 to 75. The delta ($60 to 240/month) is what "EU-incorporated, SLA-backed, dedicated-VM, decoupled-auth" costs. Per the founder's stated ranking, that is the correct trade, and it remains under 10% of the projected 10k-MAU infrastructure budget from `stack-recommendation.md`. [inferred from verified unit prices]
