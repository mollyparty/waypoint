# Waypoint Stack Validation (Second Opinion)

Version-Timestamp: 2026-07-30 17:30:00 UTC-4

Scope: a rigorous second-opinion validation of `research/05-product/stack-recommendation.md`, requested by Claudio before betting the build on it. The founder's specific doubts: (a) is Supabase really the best database and backend platform, (b) is Hetzner (or "another VPS") the right home for the routing engine, and (c) does every layer survive growth to 1M MAU without a dead end. All sources accessed 2026-07-30 unless noted. Every claim carries a [verified]/[inferred]/[assumption] tag per `research/00-RESEARCH-PLAYBOOK.md`. Prices exclude VAT unless stated; re-verify at contract time.

Executive verdict up front: no layer is REVISED. Two layers are CONFIRMED WITH CONDITIONS (Supabase and Hetzner), and the conditions are specific and cheap to implement now, expensive to retrofit later. The single most important architectural fact protecting every verdict below: Waypoint's privacy architecture keeps HealthKit and Article 9 health data on-device, so the server-side database never holds special category health data. That one design decision is what makes a $25/month Supabase project compliant where a health-data-on-server design would need a $950+/month posture or self-hosting. Guard it.

## 1. Method

What was compared, and against what criteria.

**Layers validated**: database and backend platform (deep, the founder's main doubt), compute and hosting for the routing engine and API (deep), map and navigation SDK, on-device coaching AI, subscription infrastructure, and Vercel's role (briefer passes). The GraphHopper engine choice itself was validated in the original document and is not re-litigated here; its hosting is.

**Criteria applied to every candidate**:

## 2. Database and backend platform (the founder's main doubt)

### The compliance frame that decides everything

Waypoint is not HIPAA-covered (verified in `research/01-market/regulatory-compliance.md`: not a covered entity or business associate). The binding obligations are GDPR Article 9, FTC HBNR, Washington MHMD, and Apple 5.1.3. The stack recommendation's privacy architecture keeps raw HealthKit data, readiness computation, and coaching inference on-device; the server holds account records, consent ledger, entitlements, preference weights, and privacy-zone geometries. [verified against both prior documents]

Consequence: the database platform question is not "which vendor signs a BAA" but "which vendor gives encrypted, EU-resident, DPA-covered standard Postgres with first-class PostGIS, at solo-founder ops burden." That reframing matters because HIPAA-grade postures are dramatically more expensive everywhere: Supabase's HIPAA add-on alone requires the $599/month Team plan plus a $350/month add-on. [verified] (Supabase security page confirming BAA on Team/Enterprise, https://supabase.com/security; add-on pricing per Supabase maintainer statement documented at https://axonbuild.com/blog/is-supabase-hipaa-compliant/; both accessed 2026-07-30) Waypoint does not need this as long as health data stays on-device. If that architectural line ever moves, this entire section must be re-run. [inferred, high confidence]

### Failure stories and migration post-mortems (what the founder asked for)

- **Supabase outage record.** Third-party trackers monitoring the official status page count 47 incidents in the last 90 days (10 major, median duration 2h40m) and roughly 7 incidents per month averaged since 2021; a separate tracker counts 63 outages totaling 229 hours since 2024. [verified as to the tracker numbers] (IsDown, https://isdown.app/status/supabase; DownRightNow, https://downrightnow.app/outages/service/supabase; both accessed 2026-07-30) Critical reading: the incident list is dominated by control-plane and platform services (log ingestion, dashboard, edge function deploys, management API), not the Postgres data plane; a live example on the day of writing is an Edge Function deployment incident while databases run normally, and the July 16, 2026 Frankfurt incident (projects stuck after an infrastructure provider connectivity issue) is the kind that would hurt Waypoint. [verified as to the incidents; the data-plane vs control-plane characterization is [inferred] from reading the incident titles] (Supabase status page, https://status.supabase.com/, accessed 2026-07-30)
- **Migration-away post-mortems.** The consistent lesson across practitioner write-ups: leaving the Postgres part of Supabase is trivial (pg_dump/pg_restore with --no-owner --no-acl), leaving Storage is moderate, and leaving Auth is the genuinely hard part, because users live in an `auth.users` schema you do not own and RLS policies couple to `auth.uid()`. One migration guide rates Auth "High" difficulty ("closer to a product architecture change"); another documents keeping Supabase Auth while moving everything else, verifying Supabase JWTs from the new backend via the JWKS endpoint as the standard hybrid pattern. A cost-driven post-mortem moved only the metered part (egress) and kept auth. And the most sober take: "if your Supabase bill is under two thousand dollars per month, migration almost never pays back... If you are under one hundred thousand monthly active users and have no compliance or multi-region requirement, migration is almost certainly premature." [verified as to each source's claims] (Minbook operational review with the migration difficulty table, https://minbook.dev/en/blog/railway-supabase-review/; bytelabs "When to Stop Using Supabase", https://www.bytelabs.space/blog/when-to-stop-using-supabase-for-postgres; egress migration post-mortem, https://abhijitmohanty.com/posts/migrating-second-brain-off-supabase/; WitsCode migration guide, https://witscode.com/blogs/migrate-off-supabase; all accessed 2026-07-30)
- **Neon production record.** May 2025: 5.5 hours across two days in us-east-1 where inactive databases could not be created or started (running databases unaffected; root cause a query-planner regression cascading into IP pool exhaustion, publicly post-mortemed). Four more regional incidents May to June 2026 during the rollout of their cell architecture. Neon's own production readiness guidance is to disable scale-to-zero for production, which removes the serverless pricing advantage for an always-on workload like Waypoint's. [verified] (Neon post-mortem, https://neon.com/blog/postmortem-delayed-start-compute-operations; 2026 incident table and SLA analysis, https://toolchew.com/en/review-neon-2026/; both accessed 2026-07-30)
- **PlanetScale, fresh disqualifier.** On the day of this validation, PlanetScale's own docs carry the notice that postgis, postgis_topology, postgis_sfcgal, and the address standardizers are "temporarily disabled" due to an operational issue. A platform where the moat's core extension can be switched off vendor-side is not a home for the moat. [verified] (https://planetscale.com/docs/postgres/extensions, accessed 2026-07-30)

### Verdict: CONFIRM Supabase, with five conditions

Supabase remains the right call, for the original reasons (one vendor for Postgres, PostGIS, pgrouting, auth, and API glue at $25/month) plus two new ones from this pass: the company is demonstrably durable (over $1B raised, $10.5B valuation, 250k customers as of June 2026 [verified, sources above]), and its scaling story now extends further than the original document knew (16XL compute, geo-routed read replicas, Multigres sharding in preview [verified]). Neon offers no advantage for an always-on workload and adds acquisition-direction risk; AWS is 5 to 10x the cost at this stage and adds two platforms' worth of glue work; Firebase and PlanetScale are disqualified on PostGIS grounds (Firebase structurally, PlanetScale operationally); self-managed Postgres buys nothing but founder hours at MVP. [inferred verdict on verified facts]

**The conditions (all cheap now, expensive later):**

1. **Create the project in an EU region (Frankfurt) day one.** Satisfies GDPR data residency preferences and keeps the DPIA simple. Note the July 16, 2026 Frankfurt incident: EU region does not mean immune, so condition 4 still applies. [inferred from verified compliance requirements]
2. **Keep the health-data-on-device line absolute.** It is the load-bearing wall of the compliance posture and the cost model. Any feature that wants server-side HealthKit-derived data triggers re-validation of this entire layer. [inferred]
3. **Execute Supabase's DPA and record it in the processor inventory** (compliance checklist item 9). Encryption at rest (AES-256) and in transit are platform defaults. [verified as to encryption, https://supabase.com/security, accessed 2026-07-30; DPA execution mechanics at signup are [assumption] A10]
4. **Contain the auth blast radius from day one.** This is the lock-in lesson from every post-mortem: route business logic through Waypoint's own thin API layer rather than generated client access everywhere, keep RLS policies simple, and wrap `auth.uid()` in one place. Then the proven exit (new backend verifies Supabase JWTs via JWKS, database moves first, auth moves last or never) stays a 2 to 4 week project instead of a rewrite. [inferred from the verified migration post-mortems]
5. **Buy the PITR add-on and keep an independent nightly pg_dump off-platform.** Daily backups are included on Pro; PITR is an add-on; an off-platform dump is the insurance no vendor incident can touch. [verified as to backup/PITR availability; the off-platform dump is an [inferred] recommendation]

## 3. Compute and hosting for the routing engine and API

### GraphHopper memory reality (the sizing question that drives everything)

The founder's VPS instinct is correct in kind; the question is size. Evidence:

- Official GraphHopper docs: importing the full planet requires about 60 GB RAM (about 3 hours) for the base graph, reducible to 31 GB with MMAP at the cost of days-long imports; CH preparation for a car profile with turn costs needs about 120 GB (foot and bike need much less because turn-cost support is the expensive part); the planet graph had about 415M edges as of May 2022, Europe about 150M, North America about 86M; Germany imports in about 10 minutes; and the serving heap estimate is "size of graph-cache minus the geometry file", which is far below import requirements. Import can run on a big on-demand machine and the resulting graph folder copied to smaller serving machines: this split is officially recommended. [verified] (GraphHopper deploy docs, https://github.com/graphhopper/graphhopper/blob/master/docs/core/deploy.md; technical docs, https://github.com/graphhopper/graphhopper/blob/master/docs/core/technical.md; GraphHopper blog on self-hosting the planet, https://www.graphhopper.com/blog/2022/06/27/host-your-own-worldwide-route-calculator-with-graphhopper/; planet import issue #3053 confirming 2024+ data needs more than the old figures, https://github.com/graphhopper/graphhopper/issues/3053; all accessed 2026-07-30)
- A third-party Kubernetes deployment guide claims Germany needs 40 to 60 GB "CH Graph RAM (car profile)". This conflicts with the official numbers (if the whole planet's base graph imports in 60 GB, Germany's serving footprint cannot be 40 to 60 GB) and appears to conflate peak import memory with serving memory; the same guide's own worked example serves Vietnam with three profiles in a 5 GB container. We weight the official docs and the guide's worked example over its headline table. [verified as to what each source says; the reconciliation is [inferred]] (https://tanhdev.com/posts/graphhopper-kubernetes-self-hosting-osm/, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Implication for Waypoint (foot-only profiles, no turn-cost-heavy car work): launch metros and single-country extracts serve comfortably within a 16 GB instance; a US-wide or Europe-wide foot graph is the open sizing question, bounded above by the planet figures and below by country figures. The A9 load test from the original document remains mandatory before committing to 10k-MAU instance sizes; this validation narrows the uncertainty but does not eliminate it. [inferred] (assumption A9 carried forward)

### The honest Hetzner risk (addressing the founder's VPS instinct directly)

The founder is right that this is "another VPS" territory, and that is the correct call: GraphHopper is a persistent JVM holding a large graph in memory, the textbook workload for a plain, boring, cheap VM. The real risks are not performance, they are provider-relationship and single-box risks:

- **Account risk.** Hetzner has a documented pattern of strict automated fraud prevention and abrupt account deactivations: their own FAQ warns that VPN use or free email addresses at signup can trigger rejection, and there are credible user reports of long-standing accounts suspended with data loss after payment disputes, plus Trustpilot reviews describing sudden deactivations. This is a real tail risk with a catastrophic-if-unprepared impact. [verified as to the FAQ and the existence of the reports; frequency is not quantifiable] (Hetzner fraud prevention FAQ, https://docs.hetzner.com/general/security-and-identify/fraud-prevention-faq/; Hetzner data protection FAQ on deactivation reasons, https://docs.hetzner.com/general/company-and-policy/data-protection-at-hetzner/; LowEndTalk account-suspension thread, https://lowendtalk.com/discussion/211213/5-years-with-hetzner-treated-like-sh-t-this-week; Trustpilot review pattern, https://www.trustpilot.com/review/hetzner.com; all accessed 2026-07-30)
- **No published SLA** on Hetzner Cloud, versus DigitalOcean's 99.99%. For a route generator whose failure degrades to "app cannot generate new routes" (cached routes and recorded runs still work on-device), this is acceptable at MVP if and only if recovery is rehearsed. [verified as to the SLA gap; acceptability judgment is [inferred]]
- **Single-VPS blast radius.** Mitigations are standard and cheap: nightly graph and config snapshots stored off-Hetzner (S3 or Backblaze B2), the whole box defined in infrastructure-as-code (cloud-init plus a container image), and a rehearsed restore-to-new-provider drill. Because GraphHopper serving is stateless (the graph is a build artifact), a cold rebuild on any provider is hours, not days, once the graph files are safe. [inferred, high confidence given the verified import/serve split]
- **Latency for US/UK/EU users.** Hetzner covers Germany/Finland and US East/West. Route generation tolerates hundreds of milliseconds (it is a "generate my run" tap, not a keystroke path), so a single EU node serves the whole base at MVP; add the US node when US traffic is material. US Hetzner traffic allowances are smaller (1 to 8 TB) but route responses are kilobytes, so this is a non-issue. [verified as to regions and allowances; the tolerance judgment is [inferred]]

### Verdict: CONFIRM Hetzner, with conditions

Hetzner at roughly one third of the next-cheapest credible option, for a stateless-serving workload with rehearsed recovery, is the right risk-adjusted choice for a solo founder. OVH is the named fallback (EU sovereignty, unmetered bandwidth, one command away if Hetzner relationship risk materializes). Conditions: (1) off-provider backups of graph artifacts, database dumps, and IaC from week one; (2) restore drill before beta; (3) business account with clean verification and a reliable payment method plus a calendar-checked billing contact, given the documented account-suspension pattern; (4) the A9 load test before 10k-MAU sizing commitments; (5) add the US node and a load balancer when US latency data says so, not before. [inferred verdict on verified facts]
