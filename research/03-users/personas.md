# User Personas

Version-Timestamp: 2026-07-30 15:20:00 UTC-4

**Executive summary.** Four evidence-based personas operationalize the segmentation in `segmentation.md` and the complaint evidence from Phase 2: Marcus, the committed amateur racer (core target, the proven payer whose training context makes adaptive routes defensible); Priya, the traveling professional runner (the wedge's sharpest use case, where "where do I run, right now, from here" is a several-times-a-month emergency); Elena, the safety-first city runner (for whom route constraints are existential, representing the 92 percent of women runners with safety concerns that no product accepts as a routing input); and Jake, the ambitious beginner (the Gen Z growth segment and secondary target, currently being injured by aggressive plan defaults). Every demographic and behavioral trait traces to segmentation data or documented community evidence; illustrative details are labeled as such. Together the four personas test H1 (where-to-run friction), H6 (iOS plus wearable form factor), and H7 (routine building as retention), and they feed the jobs-to-be-done document. These are desk personas: they must be pressure-tested against 8 to 12 real interviews before they harden into design targets.

**Desk research disclaimer.** No Waypoint user interviews exist yet. These personas are assembled from published surveys, race registration data, platform reports, and mined competitor complaints (Phase 2), all accessed 2026-07-30. Names, ages within sourced bands, occupations, and scenario color are illustrative composites and labeled. Interview-needed flags mark the claims that most need primary validation.

## How to read these personas

- [verified] traces to a published source or a Phase 1/2 internal document; citation inline.
- [inferred] is a reasoned conclusion from verified inputs.
- [assumption] and "illustrative" mark composite color that makes the persona usable but is not evidence.
- Traits without illustrative labels are traced to the segmentation or community evidence.

## Persona 1: Marcus, the committed amateur racer (core target)

**Essence: runs four times a week around a job and family, races a spring half and a fall marathon, coaches himself with apps, and is bored to death of his own three routes.**

- Demographics and running profile: male, late 30s (30 to 39 is the largest adult race cohort at 20.0 percent, and men are 44.8 percent of race participants [verified], RunSignup 2025 RaceTrends, https://info.runsignup.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/02/25-Race-Trends-FOR-ONLINE-compressed-1.pdf; marathoner average age is 39 [verified], Zipdo, https://zipdo.co/marathon-statistics/). College educated with household income above $75,000 (68 percent of marathoners hold a bachelor's or higher; 23 percent report household income above $100,000 [verified, medium credibility], Zipdo above; consistent with dated Running USA core-runner data [verified, dated], 5kevents.org, https://5kevents.org/demographics.html). Runs 3 to 5 times per week, races 2 to 4 times per year (segment definition, `segmentation.md` Section 3.3). Runs alone (74.7 percent of engaged runners primarily do [verified], SGB Media, https://sgbonline.com/exec-running-usas-survey-finds-hoka-lululemon-garmin-gaining-favor-with-runners/). Married, two kids, suburban edge of a mid-size city: illustrative.
- Goals: a half marathon personal best this year, staying injury-free through the build, keeping running compatible with family logistics [inferred from segment motivations: fitness 75.2 percent, stress relief 35.3 percent, SGB Media above].
- Frustrations (cited): his apps prescribe the workout but not the place; "two different runners at the same corner get the same routes" (`research/02-competitors/profile-strava-routes.md`) [verified]; generated routes have sent runners onto unsafe roads, "12 miles of very unsafe roads that were single lane, no shoulder, busy with traffic" (`research/02-competitors/gap-analysis.md`, unmet need 2) [verified]; Runna-style plans feel "borderline too fast... even a easy day was a grind" and carry injury discourse (`research/02-competitors/profile-runna.md`, complaint theme 1) [verified as sentiment]; subscription billing traps across the category erode trust (`gap-analysis.md`, unmet need 7) [verified].
- Current tools and workarounds: Apple Watch or Garmin plus Strava free or Premium, a Runna trial he may or may not have kept (Runna's payer base is exactly this user, `profile-runna.md`) [verified]; workaround for routes is rotating 2 or 3 memorized loops and occasionally hand-building one in Strava's route builder [inferred from Phase 2 product analysis; interview needed on actual route-selection habits].
- What Waypoint must do to win him: generate the day's workout as a route (tempo day gets a flat uninterrupted loop, easy day gets soft surface), from his front door, with honest effort calibration that does not push him into injury, and clean transparent billing. Post the run to Strava rather than replacing it (`gap-analysis.md`, Section d) [inferred].
- Willingness to pay: highest of the four. Benchmarks: Runna $119.99 per year, Strava $79.99, RunGo $59.99 for navigation alone (`research/01-market/market-sizing.md`, Section 5) [verified]. He already pays for at least one running subscription [assumption within the 10 to 15 percent payer share, market-sizing.md assumption 3].
- Jobs he hires an app for: prescribe today's workout; tell me where to do it; keep me from getting injured; show progress toward the race; log everything to Strava; make the daily decision effortless.

## Persona 2: Priya, the traveling professional runner (wedge's sharpest case)

**Essence: a consultant in a different city every week, mid marathon block, standing in a hotel lobby at 6 a.m. with 10 miles to do and no idea where to run them.**

- Demographics and running profile: female, early 30s, millennial (millennials are the most consistent on-trip exercisers, 46 percent working out on every or almost every trip [verified], Smart Meetings/Amex-GBTA, https://www.smartmeetings.com/news/hotels-resorts/97407/business-travelers-hotel-gym). Committed amateur by frequency (3 to 5 runs per week, `segmentation.md` Section 3.3). High income professional services role: illustrative but consistent with the affluent skew of both runners and frequent business travelers [inferred]. 60+ hotel nights per year: illustrative.
- Goals: hold her training plan together across time zones and unfamiliar cities; finish her goal race despite the travel load; use runs to see the cities she works in [inferred; run-travel intent is mainstream, with one in two Australians planning run travel in 2025 [verified], Travel Weekly AU, https://travelweekly.com.au/skyscanner-reveals-surge-in-run-travel-for-2025/].
- Frustrations (cited): 83 percent of business travelers weigh fitness options when booking hotels, yet only 2 in 10 manage regular exercise on trips, with schedule (71 percent) and energy (47 percent) as top blockers [verified] (Hotel News Resource/GBTA, https://mobi.hotelnewsresource.com/?p=106337; BCD Travel, https://www.bcdtravel.com/resources/traveler-wellbeing-get-moving/). Existing tools fail her exact question: coaching apps have zero route capability, route apps have zero training context, and Strava's suggestions rank by local popularity she has no relationship with (`research/02-competitors/gap-analysis.md`, unmet need 1) [verified]. Researching a safe hotel-door route eats the time the run needed [inferred; interview needed on her actual pre-trip research ritual].
- Current tools and workarounds: hotel treadmill as the default surrender; asking hotel front desks or colleagues; searching "running routes near me"; scanning Strava heatmaps the night before; repeating the one route she knows in each recurring city [inferred from the documented tool gaps; interview needed]. The founder's own experience matches this pattern (`vault/01-Project/Founder-Brief.md`) [verified as founder testimony, not market data].
- What Waypoint must do to win her: a trustworthy route from the hotel door in under 30 seconds, sized to today's workout, honest about safety and lighting at 6 a.m., with turn-by-turn watch guidance so she never stops to check a phone in an unfamiliar city (`gap-analysis.md`, unmet need 8) [inferred].
- Willingness to pay: high. She already spends to protect routine on the road (hotel choice is fitness-driven for 83 percent [verified]), and the alternative cost (a lost long run mid-block) is what she is buying insurance against [inferred]. Race travel spend corroborates: 59 percent of marathon travelers stay 3 to 5 nights [verified] (Euronews, https://www.euronews.com/travel/2025/02/03/are-race-cations-on-your-bucket-list-the-trend-for-marathon-tourism-is-running-away-with-i).
- Jobs she hires an app for: answer "where do I run, right now, from here"; keep the plan intact while traveling; keep me safe in a city I do not know; guide me hands-free; make the run a way to experience the city; never make me think the night before.

## Persona 3: Elena, the safety-first city runner (constraints are existential)

**Essence: an urban runner who plans every run around daylight, lighting, and escape routes before she ever thinks about pace, because for her the route IS the safety decision.**

- Demographics and running profile: female, late 20s (women are 53 percent of US race participants [verified], RunSignup above; women drove the recent UK running resurgence, up 250,000 year over year [verified], Sport England Active Lives Nov 2023-24 report, https://sportengland-production-files.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/s3fs-public/2025-04/ActiveLivesAdult-Nov23-24_V9-23-04-25-10-03-03-02.pdf). Runs 3 to 4 times per week, often before work or after dark by necessity of schedule: illustrative timing, evidence-consistent. City apartment dweller: illustrative.
- The evidence that makes her existential, not niche: 92 percent of women runners report safety concerns on the run; 51 percent fear physical attack; 38 percent have experienced physical or verbal harassment; 69 percent take specific precautions [verified] (Adidas survey of 9,000 runners across nine countries, via Runner's World UK, https://www.runnersworld.com/uk/news/a43270444/women-concerned-for-safety-when-they-run/, and Runner's World, https://www.runnersworld.com/news/a43276321/adidas-womens-safety-initiative/). In North West England, 68 percent of 498 surveyed women runners experienced abuse while running and only 5 percent reported it; 82 percent hold safety concerns and take multiple mitigation measures [verified] (University of Manchester, https://blog.policy.manchester.ac.uk/posts/2024/11/the-abuse-of-women-runners-implications-for-the-violence-against-women-and-girls-policy-agenda/; The Guardian, https://www.theguardian.com/world/2024/feb/22/two-thirds-of-women-get-harassed-while-running-england-survey-finds). A 2021 Runner's World survey found 60 percent of women runners had experienced harassment [verified] (cited in the Manchester briefing above).
- Goals: run consistently without fear tax; stop shrinking her running world to two proven loops; eventually train for a 10K without route anxiety dictating her schedule [inferred from the harassment research's documented behavior changes].
- Frustrations (cited): no product in fifteen-plus examined accepts safety, lighting, or time of day as a routing input; Strava's Night Heatmap is a display layer, not a constraint; users explicitly ask for time-of-day awareness and dangerous-route flagging and are unanswered (`research/02-competitors/gap-analysis.md`, unmet need 2; `profile-strava-routes.md`) [verified]. Generic generated routes can send her somewhere isolated precisely because no engine knows the difference [inferred].
- Current tools and workarounds: documented precaution patterns include running with others, sharing live location, carrying keys between fingers, tying hair up, and restricting to familiar well-populated routes [verified as patterns] (Adidas survey above, 69 percent take precautions; Manchester study above). App-side: live location sharing (Strava Beacon or Find My), plus manual route vetting on foot or by car [inferred; interview needed on which digital tools she actually trusts].
- What Waypoint must do to win her: treat lighting, foot traffic, open businesses, and time of day as first-class routing constraints, not a mode; explain WHY a route was chosen; never leak her home location or live position (route privacy is a Phase 1 compliance headline, `research/01-market/regulatory-compliance.md` via `_index.md`) [verified for the compliance stakes]; degrade gracefully by saying "no good route meets your constraints right now" rather than pretending [inferred]. Marketing must avoid fear-based framing [assumption on brand ethics, flagged for founder decision].
- Willingness to pay: real but complicated. She is being failed at a need she cannot opt out of, which supports payment; but charging a safety premium to women raises an ethical and brand problem the founder must decide deliberately (safety-critical constraints in the free tier is the defensible default) [inferred; decision needed, flag for `vault/02-Decisions/`].
- Jobs she hires an app for: find me a route where I will feel and be safe at this hour; widen my running world beyond my two proven loops; let someone I trust follow my run live; never expose where I live; give me confidence in a new neighborhood or city.

## Persona 4: Jake, the ambitious beginner (growth segment, secondary target)

**Essence: nine months into running via a run club and a couch-to-5K app, signed up for his first half marathon on a dare, motivated, overconfident, and one aggressive training plan away from a shin splint.**

- Demographics and running profile: male or female, early-to-mid 20s (18 to 29 is 17.9 percent of 2025 race participants and rising, 21.8 percent in H1 2026 [verified], RunSignup 2025 RaceTrends and 2026 Midyear Update, https://info.runsignup.com/2026/06/22/2026-midyear-race-trends-update/). Race-motivated (Gen Z is 75 percent more likely than Gen X to cite a race as their main exercise motivation [verified], Strava Year in Sport 2025, https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-2025). Found running through a run club (running clubs grew 3.5x on Strava in 2025 [verified], same source). Runs 2 to 4 times per week, first race done or imminent (segment definition, `segmentation.md` Section 3.1). Entry-level job, city, mixes running with lifting: illustrative, consistent with Gen Z multi-sport patterns [verified for the pattern, endurance.biz Year in Sport summary, https://endurance.biz/2025/featured-news/stravas-12th-annual-year-in-sport-report-gen-z-doomscrolling-is-out-movement-is-in/].
- Goals: finish the first half marathon; look and feel like a real runner; keep the streak and the club identity going; a personal best to post [verified as segment pattern: 86 percent of Runna-connected runners hit a personal best in 2025, Strava above].
- Frustrations (cited): plans calibrated for people who overstate fitness hurt beginners most; Runna's complaint record includes stress fractures and shin splints attributed to aggressive defaults and "too little easy running for beginners" (`research/02-competitors/profile-runna.md`, complaint themes 1 and 4) [verified as sentiment]; the conservative alternative caps ambition with 10 to 12 minute runs (`profile-trainasone.md` via `gap-analysis.md`, unmet need 4) [verified]; twice as many Gen Z as Gen X say picking up a new sport is intimidating [verified] (endurance.biz above); and he runs the same park loop every time because he does not know where else is good [inferred; interview needed].
- Current tools and workarounds: free stack: Couch to 5K graduate (7 million downloads since 2016, 790,000 in 2024 [verified], Floom Creative, https://floomcreative.co.uk/uk-running-boom-2026/), Strava free for the club feed, Apple Watch or a first Garmin (Gen Z cites wearables as its biggest fitness investment 63 percent more often than Gen X [verified], endurance.biz above), parkrun on Saturdays where available (18.2 million global finishes in 2025 [verified], parkrun US, https://www.parkrun.us/collegepark/news/2025/12/).
- What Waypoint must do to win him: gentle honest calibration that protects him from his own ambition (the unclaimed middle ground between Runna-aggressive and TrainAsONE-conservative, `gap-analysis.md`, unmet need 4); routes that make new-runner distances feel like exploration, not laps; visible progress and shareable moments; a free tier that is genuinely useful before the race commitment justifies paying [inferred].
- Willingness to pay: medium and event-driven. 30 percent of Gen Z plan to spend more on fitness in 2026 [verified] (endurance.biz above), and beginners are already 26 percent of Runna's paying funnel [verified] (Strava above), but conversion is trial-heavy and churns after the goal race [assumption; RevenueCat-style cohort validation needed].
- Jobs they hire an app for: get me to the finish line uninjured; tell me what "easy" actually means for me; make runs feel like progress and adventure; give me things to share with the club; do not make me feel slow.

## Persona-to-hypothesis mapping

| Persona | H1: where-to-run friction drives adoption | H6: iOS-first with wearables is right | H7: routine building deepens retention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marcus (committed amateur racer) | Tests the base rate: friction is real but episodic at home, spikes on travel and route boredom. If Marcus shrugs at "where", H1 fails for the core segment | Strong test: Apple Watch is Strava's top device [verified]; but his possible Garmin loyalty stresses the HealthKit-first plan (Garmin program paused, Phase 1) | Primary test: his self-coached plan-following is exactly the routine layer; retention depends on it |
| Priya (traveling professional) | Sharpest test: friction is acute, frequent, and unserved. If H1 holds anywhere, it holds here first | Supports: iPhone plus watch is the travel kit; offline and watch-only guidance are the form-factor stress cases | Moderate test: routine that survives schedule chaos is her version of H7 |
| Elena (safety-first runner) | Reframes H1: her friction is "where is SAFE to run", a stronger form of the same job. Tests whether safety constraints alone drive adoption | Neutral to supportive: watch live-location sharing is a safety feature in itself | Weak test: routine matters less than constraint trust; she retains on safety reliability |
| Jake (ambitious beginner) | Weak-to-moderate test: friction is "where is good", curiosity more than pain. Tests the free-tier acquisition surface | Supports: Gen Z wearable investment is documented [verified] | Second primary test: calibrated progression uninjured to race day is the H7 promise for the growth segment |

Reading: H1 lives or dies with Priya-type moments in Marcus-type lives; H6 is broadly supported with the Garmin wildcard; H7 is carried by Marcus and Jake. This table feeds `jobs-to-be-done.md` and Phase 4's `hypotheses-review.md`.

## Assumptions

- [assumption] Persona ages, occupations, genders, and life circumstances are composites chosen to sit at the center of sourced demographic bands; real interview subjects will redistribute them.
- [assumption] Priya's travel frequency (60+ hotel nights) and the frequent-runner x frequent-traveler intersection size are unsourced; no dataset cross-tabulates running frequency with travel frequency.
- [assumption] Workaround descriptions (route vetting rituals, hotel treadmill surrender, park-loop repetition) are inferred from tool-gap analysis and scattered community evidence, not from systematic observation.
- [assumption] Elena's willingness to pay treats safety-critical routing as free-tier; this is a product-ethics judgment, not evidence, and needs a founder decision record.
- [assumption] Jake's post-race churn pattern is extrapolated from category subscription behavior, not from beginner-runner cohort data.
- [assumption] Complaint evidence inherited from Phase 2 (Runna injuries, Strava dangerous routes) is treated as perception fact, not adjudicated product truth, per the Phase 2 caveats.

## Open questions

1. Does Marcus actually experience route boredom and route friction as pain, or only as mild inconvenience? This is the H1 base-rate question and the top interview priority [interview needed: 8 to 12 committed amateurs, including at least 4 who travel monthly].
2. What does Priya's night-before ritual actually look like, and what would she pay to delete it? [interview needed: 3 to 5 frequent-traveler runners].
3. Which digital safety tools do women runners trust today, and what would make an algorithmic "safe route" claim credible rather than alarming? [interview needed: 4 to 6 women runners across urban densities; consider a specialist moderator given the topic's sensitivity].
4. Does Jake want coaching or community first? The run-club evidence suggests identity and belonging may outrank training quality for this segment [interview needed; also test free-tier feature ranking].
5. Do any personas overlap in one person strongly enough to merge (Priya is plausibly Marcus-who-travels; Elena's constraints apply to all women across the other three personas)? Interviews should test whether safety is a persona or a cross-cutting layer [interview needed].
6. Wearable split among target users (Apple Watch vs Garmin) directly gates H6 scope; get real numbers before committing the v1 integration surface [interview or survey needed].
