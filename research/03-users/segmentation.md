# Runner Segmentation

Version-Timestamp: 2026-07-30 15:20:00 UTC-4

**Executive summary.** The runner population divides by commitment into five behavioral segments: beginners (the fastest-growing inflow, powered by Gen Z and structured pipelines like Couch to 5K and parkrun), casual lifestyle runners (the largest segment and the engine of 2025 participation growth), committed amateurs (3 to 5 runs per week, a few races per year, roughly 25 percent of runners and the segment that demonstrably pays for running apps), serious racers (small, demanding, often already coached), and returners (real but nearly invisible in published data). The founder's committed-amateur hypothesis survives a stress test with one important nuance: the US "dedicated core" softened slightly in 2025 (down 1.1 percent per OIA) while casual and beginner participation boomed, which means the committed amateur is the right segment to monetize today but a poor segment to bet on for volume growth alone. Recommendation: primary segment stays the committed amateur (highest willingness to pay, validated by Runna's roughly 90,000 payers, and the only segment whose training context makes adaptive routes clearly better than free loops); secondary segment is the ambitious beginner (the growth pipeline that matures into the primary segment, already 26 percent of Runna's own base), with product design that serves them without a separate product.

**Desk research disclaimer.** Everything below is desk research from published surveys, industry reports, platform data, and prior Waypoint research phases, accessed 2026-07-30. No Waypoint user interviews have been conducted. Segment boundaries, sizing percentages, and willingness-to-pay attributions are directional and must be validated in Phase 3 interviews before they drive product or pricing decisions. Interview-needed flags appear inline and in Open questions.

## 1. Method and definitions

- Segmentation axis: commitment and behavior (frequency, race participation, training structure), not demographics. Demographics are described per segment where sourceable.
- Population base: 52.3 million Americans ran on the road in 2025 (ran at least once, ages 6+) [verified] (Outdoor Industry Association, "2026 Running Report Executive Summary", https://oia.outdoorindustry.org/l/51282/2026-04-21/f4v3sc, April 2026, accessed 2026-07-30). Trail running adds 17.2 million, up 6.6 percent year over year and 57 percent since 2019 [verified] (same source). England: 7.1 million adults running, up over 500,000 in a year [verified] (Sport England, "England is getting more active but not everyone is benefiting", Active Lives Adult Survey Nov 2024 to Nov 2025, https://www.sportengland.org/news-and-inspiration/england-getting-more-active-not-everyone-benefiting, accessed 2026-07-30). Five-geography total used in Phase 1: roughly 110 million runners (see `research/01-market/market-sizing.md`, Section 3.1).
- "Core" vs "casual": SFIA defines CORE participants as those who account for the majority of play occasions for an activity [verified] (SFIA, "2026 Topline Participation Report" press release, https://sfia.org/resources/participation-hits-new-high-but-majority-of-americans-not-yet-meeting-recommended-guidelines-of-150-minutes-of-weekly-activity-sfias-2026-topline-report-finds/, 2026-03-12, accessed 2026-07-30). OIA applies this split to running: casual runners are growing about 7 percent per year while core running slipped 1.1 percent [verified] (OIA 2026 Running Report, above).
- Segment percentage sizing below is a judgment layer over these verified totals; each percentage is tagged.

## 2. Segment overview

| Segment | Working definition | US size estimate | Growth direction | Willingness-to-pay signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Beginner / couch-to-5K | Running under 2 years, 1 to 3 runs per week, first race not yet done or just done | 15 to 20 percent of runners, 8 to 10M [assumption] | Strongly up | Low to medium, trial-heavy |
| 2. Casual lifestyle runner | 1 to 3 runs per week folded into a multi-sport routine, races rarely | 40 to 45 percent, 21 to 24M [assumption] | Up ~7 percent per year [verified, OIA] | Low |
| 3. Committed amateur | 3 to 5 runs per week, races a few times per year, no human coach | ~25 percent, ~13M US, ~27.5M across five geographies [assumption, from Phase 1] | Flat to slightly down [verified, OIA core -1.1 percent] | Highest, proven |
| 4. Serious racer / club competitor | 5 to 7+ runs per week, structured training, club or coach relationship | 5 to 10 percent, 3 to 5M [assumption] | Flat | High but often already spent (coach, club, TrainingPeaks) |
| 5. Returner | Previously committed, lapsed 6+ months, restarting | Overlaps others; not separately measured in any found source | Unknown | Unknown [interview needed] |

Percentages do not sum to 100 because returners overlap the other four and because the boundaries are behavioral bands, not census categories. The 25 percent committed-amateur figure is the Phase 1 assumption (market-sizing.md, assumption 1) carried forward for consistency; nothing found in this pass contradicts it, and Running USA's finding that 53.3 percent of its race-adjacent survey respondents self-identify as "frequent/fitness runners" brackets it from above, since that sample skews heavily toward engaged racers [verified for the survey figure; inferred for the bracketing] (SGB Media, "EXEC: Running USA's Survey Finds Hoka, Lululemon, Garmin Gaining Favor with Runners", https://sgbonline.com/exec-running-usas-survey-finds-hoka-lululemon-garmin-gaining-favor-with-runners/, accessed 2026-07-30).

## 3. Segment detail

### 3.1 Beginner / couch-to-5K

- Behavior: 1 to 3 runs per week, short distances, high dropout risk, motivated by health and increasingly by a race goal. Gen Z is 75 percent more likely than Gen X to say a race or event is their main exercise motivation [verified] (Strava, "12th Annual Year In Sport Trend Report", https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-2025, 2025-12-03, accessed 2026-07-30).
- Pipeline evidence: NHS Couch to 5K has 7 million downloads since 2016, 790,000 in 2024 alone [verified] (Floom Creative, "The UK Running Boom 2026", https://floomcreative.co.uk/uk-running-boom-2026/, accessed 2026-07-30, citing NHS data; secondary source, medium credibility). parkrun recorded 18.2 million 5K finishes globally in 2025, up 14.4 percent over 2024 [verified] (parkrun US, "Report 421: 2025 in Review", https://www.parkrun.us/collegepark/news/2025/12/, accessed 2026-07-30). UK parkrun set a single-day participation record in May 2025 with over 230,000 participants and 33,378 new registrations that week [verified] (runABC, "parkrun myths dispelled 2025", https://runabc.co.uk/parkrun-myths-dispelled-2025-rt, accessed 2026-07-30).
- Demographics: young-adult race participation rebounded, 17.9 percent of US race participants aged 18 to 29 in 2025 and 21.8 percent in the first half of 2026 [verified] (RunSignup, "2025 RaceTrends Report", https://info.runsignup.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/02/25-Race-Trends-FOR-ONLINE-compressed-1.pdf, and "2026 Midyear Race Trends Update", https://info.runsignup.com/2026/06/22/2026-midyear-race-trends-update/, both accessed 2026-07-30).
- App behavior: beginners are already inside the paid coaching funnel; 26 percent of Runna users self-rate as beginners and 34 percent as intermediate [verified] (Strava Year in Sport 2025, above). Free anchors dominate (Couch to 5K, Nike Run Club, parkrun).
- Willingness to pay: medium at best; trial-heavy, churn-prone; the complaint record shows beginners are the group most burned by aggressive plan defaults (Runna injury discourse, `research/02-competitors/profile-runna.md`, complaint themes 1 and 4) [verified as sentiment pattern].

### 3.2 Casual lifestyle runner

- Behavior: running is one activity in a mix; 54 percent of Strava users now track multiple activity types [verified] (endurance.biz, "Strava's 12th annual Year in Sport report", https://endurance.biz/2025/featured-news/stravas-12th-annual-year-in-sport-report-gen-z-doomscrolling-is-out-movement-is-in/, accessed 2026-07-30). OIA describes 2025 growth as "casual, lower-frequency runners who fold running into a wider mix of fitness and outdoor activities" [verified] (OIA 2026 Running Report, above).
- Sizing: the largest single band; residual after the other segments [inferred].
- Races: rare, and when they race it is a 5K; 62 percent of 5K registrants sign up within the last month [verified] (RunSignup 2025 RaceTrends, above).
- Willingness to pay: low; this is the free-tier population and the zero-price anchor documented in Phase 2 (`research/02-competitors/pricing-matrix.md` context; free generators and Nike Run Club serve them adequately) [inferred].

### 3.3 Committed amateur (the founder's target)

- Behavior: 3 to 5 runs per week, a few races per year across 5K to marathon, self-coached with app support. Running USA's engaged-runner sample: 74.7 percent primarily run alone, top motivations are staying in shape (75.2 percent), stress relief (35.3 percent), enjoyment (34 percent), and 62.9 percent have run for 10+ years [verified for the survey population, which skews toward this segment and segment 4] (SGB Media, above).
- Demographics: race-field data is the best proxy. 53 percent of US race participants are women [verified] (RunSignup 2025 RaceTrends, above). Age concentration: 30 to 39 is the largest adult band at 20.0 percent, with 40 to 49 at 17.5 percent [verified] (same). Marathoners average age 39; 68 percent hold a bachelor's degree or higher; 23 percent report household income above $100,000 versus 14 percent of the US population [verified as published aggregate, medium credibility] (Zipdo, "Marathon Statistics 2026", https://zipdo.co/marathon-statistics/, accessed 2026-07-30). Older Running USA core-runner data points the same direction: roughly 79 percent college educated, 69 to 76 percent with household income above $75,000 [verified as published, dated (2009 to 2013 era), directional only] (5kevents.org, "Runner Demographics", https://5kevents.org/demographics.html, accessed 2026-07-30). UK: activity participation skews affluent, 73.2 percent of the most affluent adults active versus 53.8 percent of the least [verified] (Sport England Active Lives Nov 2024-25, above).
- App usage and willingness to pay: this is the paying segment. Runna reached roughly 90,000 payers at $119.99 per year by 2025 selling to exactly this user (`research/01-market/market-sizing.md`, Section 4; `research/02-competitors/profile-runna.md`) [verified as reported]. Strava converts roughly 4 percent of 180 million registered users at $79.99 per year, and its payers concentrate among engaged athletes [inferred] (Trail Waves, "Inside the Business of Strava's $2.2B Fitness Empire", https://trailwaves.substack.com/p/inside-the-business-of-stravas-22b, accessed 2026-07-30; Business of Apps, "Strava Revenue and Usage Statistics 2026", https://www.businessofapps.com/data/strava-statistics/, accessed 2026-07-30). RunGo proves this segment pays $59.99 per year for route navigation alone (`research/02-competitors/profile-footpath-rungo.md`) [verified].
- Growth caveat: this is the segment inside OIA's softening "dedicated core" (down 1.1 percent) [verified]. See Section 6.

### 3.4 Serious racer / club competitor

- Behavior: high frequency, structured blocks, often a club, sometimes a paid human coach, which places them outside the founder's "will not pay a human coach" boundary by definition.
- Sizing proxy: marathon-and-shorter registrations on RunSignup totaled 316,759 in 2025 (RunSignup estimates it covers about 60 percent of the US endurance market, implying roughly 500,000+ US marathon registrations) [verified for the RunSignup figure; inferred for the extrapolation] (RunSignup 2025 RaceTrends, above). Marathoners register early (53 percent more than 3 months out) and repeat rarely (12.8 percent same-race return) [verified] (same).
- Willingness to pay: high in absolute terms but already allocated (club fees, coaching, TrainingPeaks-class tools); they are also the most demanding on data fidelity [inferred]. Not a launch segment.

### 3.5 Returner

- Definition: previously committed runners restarting after injury, parenthood, relocation, or life disruption.
- Evidence status: no credible published sizing found in this pass. Indirect signals only: race repeat rates are low (17.2 percent same-race return year over year, RunSignup above), Runna's August 2026 Post-Race Recovery Plans revamp targets the post-race lapse moment (`research/02-competitors/profile-runna.md`) [verified], and injury attrition is a persistent theme in the Runna complaint record [verified as sentiment]. Sizing and behavior are an acknowledged evidence gap [interview needed: prevalence, restart triggers, and app re-adoption behavior of lapsed runners].
- Product relevance: returners are likely committed amateurs in temperament with beginner-like current fitness, which is exactly the calibration gap Phase 2 found unclaimed (Runna too aggressive, TrainAsONE too conservative, `research/02-competitors/gap-analysis.md`, unmet need 4) [inferred].

## 4. Cross-segment behavior: apps and payment

- Device context for H6: Apple Watch has been the most popular device on Strava since 2024 [verified] (Business of Apps, above). Gen Z cites wearables as their biggest fitness investment 63 percent more often than Gen X [verified] (endurance.biz Year in Sport summary, above). 30 percent of Gen Z plan to spend more on fitness in 2026 [verified] (same).
- Payment reality check: median download-to-paid conversion for health and fitness apps is 2.7 percent, top decile 12.1 percent (RevenueCat, cited in `research/01-market/market-sizing.md`) [verified]. An enthusiast-skewed Android Authority reader poll found 31 percent paying for Strava, illustrating how engagement concentrates payment [verified as poll result, heavy selection bias] (Android Authority, "Survey reveals a surprising amount of you pay for Strava", https://www.androidauthority.com/pay-for-strava-poll-result-3638728/, accessed 2026-07-30).
- Reading: payment concentrates in segments 3 and 4; segments 1 and 2 convert at generic app-store rates [inferred].

## 5. Travel behavior by segment

- Race travel is mainstream in the racing segments: Running USA's 2024 survey found location is the top factor in race choice [verified] (Euronews, "Are race-cations on your bucket list?", https://www.euronews.com/travel/2025/02/03/are-race-cations-on-your-bucket-list-the-trend-for-marathon-tourism-is-running-away-with-i, 2025-02-03, accessed 2026-07-30). 59 percent of marathon travelers stay 3 to 5 nights [verified] (same). 46 percent of Tokyo Marathon 2025 participants were international [verified] (HotelBank, "The growing wave of tabi-run in Japan", https://en.hotelbank.jp/inbound/the-growing-wave-of-tabi-run-in-japan-a-new-travel-style-combining-tourism-and-running/, accessed 2026-07-30, citing Skyscanner Japan).
- Run-travel intent is broad: one in two Australians planned run travel in 2025 [verified] (Travel Weekly AU, "Skyscanner reveals surge in run travel for 2025", https://travelweekly.com.au/skyscanner-reveals-surge-in-run-travel-for-2025/, accessed 2026-07-30); 63.8 percent of running-interested respondents in the US, China, and South Korea planned a running trip in 2025 [verified] (HotelBank, above).
- Business travel: 83 percent of business travelers consider workout facilities or walkable areas when booking a hotel [verified] (Hotel News Resource, "New Business Traveler Sentiment Index", GBTA/RoomIt, https://mobi.hotelnewsresource.com/?p=106337, accessed 2026-07-30). Only 2 in 10 exercise regularly on trips, though nearly half sometimes do; the top blockers are schedule (71 percent) and energy (47 percent) [verified] (BCD Travel, "Traveler wellbeing: Get moving", https://www.bcdtravel.com/resources/traveler-wellbeing-get-moving/, accessed 2026-07-30; Smart Meetings, "Who Works Out in the Hotel Gym, and Who Doesn't", Amex/GBTA, https://www.smartmeetings.com/news/hotels-resorts/97407/business-travelers-hotel-gym, accessed 2026-07-30). Millennials are the most consistent on-trip exercisers (46 percent work out on every or almost every trip) [verified] (Smart Meetings, above).
- Gen Z travel signal: Strava reports Gen Z "putting movement first, even while on vacation" [verified] (Strava Year in Sport 2025, above).
- Segment mapping: the committed amateur and serious racer travel for races and cannot skip training runs mid-block while traveling for work or leisure; that intersection (frequent runner x frequent traveler) is where "where do I run" friction is most acute and least served [inferred]. No source cross-tabulates running frequency with travel frequency [interview needed: how often committed amateurs run in unfamiliar places, and what they do today].

## 6. Stress test: growth is beginner-led while the core softened

The Phase 1 finding holds and sharpens: US road running added over a million participants in 2025, but growth came from casual, lower-frequency runners (up about 7 percent per year) while core running slipped 1.1 percent [verified] (OIA 2026 Running Report, above). Meanwhile the youth and Gen Z race rebound is real (17.9 percent of 2025 race participants aged 18 to 29, rising to 21.8 percent in H1 2026; run clubs up 3.5x on Strava to help drive 1 million total clubs) [verified] (RunSignup and Strava sources above).

What this means for the committed-amateur target:

1. The target segment is not growing, but it is not the acquisition problem: it is the monetization base. Every proven payer benchmark in this category (Runna's 90,000 payers, RunGo's $59.99 navigation subscribers, Strava's conversion concentration) sits in this segment [verified inputs, inferred conclusion].
2. The pipeline into the segment is the healthiest it has been in a decade. Today's race-motivated Gen Z beginner is the committed amateur of 2028; Runna's own base is 60 percent beginner-plus-intermediate, showing the paid funnel already starts before commitment [verified inputs, inferred conclusion].
3. The risk to the founder's choice is timing, not direction: a product priced and designed only for today's committed amateur fights over a flat pool against Runna's brand; a product that also onboards the ambitious beginner rides the growth wave and grows its own future payers [inferred].
4. A contrarian reading must be flagged: if the core keeps shrinking, the segment thesis weakens each year; OIA's 2027 report is the check-in [assumption that the 2025 softening is cyclical, not structural].

## 7. Recommendation

- **Primary segment: the committed amateur** (3 to 5 runs per week, races a few times per year, self-coached). Rationale: only segment with proven category willingness to pay at the $60 to $120 per year level; training context is what makes adaptive route generation defensible versus free loop generators; their travel and race behavior activates the wedge's sharpest use case; Runna's documented injury and trust gaps give a differentiation seam (`research/02-competitors/gap-analysis.md`, unmet need 4). The founder's H6 target survives Phase 3 desk evidence.
- **Secondary segment: the ambitious beginner** (race-motivated, Gen Z-weighted, club-curious). Rationale: fastest-growing inflow, rebounding race participation, already inside paid coaching funnels, and structurally underserved on calibration (aggressive defaults injure them). They mature into the primary segment. Serve them with the same product through gentler defaults and safety-forward routing, not a separate SKU [inferred].
- Explicitly deprioritized: casual lifestyle runners (free-tier population, zero-price anchor), serious racers (already coached, demanding, small), returners (insufficient evidence; revisit after interviews).

## Assumptions

- [assumption] Segment percentage sizing (Section 2) layers judgment over verified participation totals; the 25 percent committed-amateur share is inherited from Phase 1 for consistency and remains unvalidated by any direct frequency-distribution source.
- [assumption] Race-field demographics (RunSignup) are a valid proxy for the committed amateur and serious racer segments; they undercount committed runners who do not race through RunSignup events.
- [assumption] Marathoner income and education figures (Zipdo aggregate, dated Running USA data) generalize directionally to the committed amateur; both sources are medium-to-low credibility for current-year precision.
- [assumption] The OIA core softening (-1.1 percent) is cyclical rather than structural; if 2026 to 2027 data shows continued decline, the primary-segment bet needs re-examination.
- [assumption] Willingness-to-pay attributions per segment are inferred from platform-level conversion data and competitor payer bases, not from segment-level payment surveys.

## Open questions

1. What share of runners actually run 3 to 5 times per week? A frequency distribution from SFIA's single-sport running report or Running USA's full dataset (both paywalled) would replace the 25 percent assumption [purchase or interview needed].
2. How often do committed amateurs run in unfamiliar locations, and what do they do today when they must? No source cross-tabulates running frequency with travel; this is the H1 make-or-break number [interview needed].
3. How large is the returner population and what triggers restart? Invisible in every published source found [interview needed].
4. Do beginners' willingness-to-pay and churn differ enough from committed amateurs to require different pricing? RevenueCat cohort data plus Waypoint's own paywall tests will answer this [test needed].
5. Does the Gen Z race-motivation surge convert into sustained 3-to-5-runs-per-week behavior, or does it churn after the goal race? Watch RunSignup repeat rates for the 18-29 cohort in the 2026 full-year report [monitor].
6. Women are 53 percent of race participants and drove the UK resurgence; does the safety gap (see `personas.md`) suppress their app spend or redirect it? [interview needed].
