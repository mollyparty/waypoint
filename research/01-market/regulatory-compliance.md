# Regulatory and Compliance Landscape for Waypoint (AI Running Coach with Adaptive Route Generation)

Version-Timestamp: 2026-07-30 14:05:00 UTC-4

Waypoint's highest-risk compliance surfaces are, in order: (1) precise location data, because generated running routes anchored at a home start point reveal home address and daily patterns, a category regulators now treat as sensitive in nearly every target market and one where design failures have produced public incidents (Strava heatmap, Flyby); (2) health and fitness data, which is GDPR Article 9 special category data, is covered by Washington's My Health My Data Act and the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule despite HIPAA not applying, and is subject to Apple's strictest App Store rules; and (3) AI transparency and claims, because the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations apply from August 2, 2026 (days after this document's date) and the FTC actively polices unsubstantiated AI marketing claims. Accessibility (European Accessibility Act, in force since June 2025) and subscription law (state auto-renewal laws plus EU withdrawal rights) are secondary but must be built in from day one because retrofitting is expensive.

> Note: this document is market and regulatory research compiled from public sources. It is not legal advice. Every legal obligation described here must be confirmed with qualified counsel in each target market before launch.

## 1. Location data privacy (highest-risk surface)

### Why routes are uniquely sensitive

- The 2018 Strava global heatmap incident showed that aggregated, "anonymized" GPS traces exposed secret military bases, patrol routes, and patterns of life in conflict zones; even aggregation is not a privacy defense when user density is low. [verified] (Ars Technica, "Heatmap for social athlete's app reveals secret bases, secret places", https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2018/01/heatmap-for-social-athletes-app-reveals-secret-bases-secret-places/, accessed 2026-07-30; BBC News, "Fitness app Strava lights up staff at military bases", https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-42853072, accessed 2026-07-30)
- NRK journalists exploited Strava's Flyby feature with fake GPS tracks to deanonymize 18 military personnel by name within hours. Lesson: any social or proximity feature layered on GPS data creates a deanonymization vector. [verified] (NRKbeta, "How we found the identity of military personnel using Strava", https://nrkbeta.no/2018/01/31/how-we-found-the-identity-of-military-personnel-using-strava/, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Strava's mitigations (privacy zones around home, opt-out of heatmap aggregation, private-by-default activities) are now the de facto industry standard that regulators and reviewers expect from any route-generating app. [inferred from the sources above and Strava's published privacy controls]
- Waypoint-specific implication: a generated route that starts and ends at the user's home is functionally a home address disclosure. Route generation must treat the start point itself as sensitive data, not just the recorded trace. [inferred]

### GDPR (EU, UK analog)

- GPS traces and planned routes are personal data; location data is explicitly listed in the GDPR Article 4(1) definition of personal data. Location data is not automatically Article 9 special category data, but it becomes special category data when it reveals health information or other protected characteristics (for example, regular visits to a clinic). [verified] (GDPR Article 4(1); analysis in Pennington Manches Cooper, "GDPR in sport: trying wearables on for size", https://www.penningtonslaw.com/insights/gdpr-in-sport-trying-wearables-on-for-size/, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Core GDPR obligations that bite for Waypoint. [verified as to the articles cited; the DPIA trigger for Waypoint's specific processing is [inferred] but strongly supported by Article 35(3)(b) criteria, which name large-scale processing of special categories]:
  - Lawful basis per purpose: consent or contract for route generation; explicit consent for anything touching Article 9 health data.
  - Purpose limitation and data minimization (Article 5(1)(b) and 5(1)(c)) and storage limitation (Article 5(1)(e)).
  - Data protection by design and by default (Article 25).
  - A Data Protection Impact Assessment (Article 35), because systematic processing of location plus health data at scale is likely "high risk".
  - Data Processing Agreements (Article 28) with every processor: cloud, analytics, maps provider, AI model host.
- An EU representative (Article 27) is required if Waypoint has no EU establishment, and a UK representative similarly under UK GDPR. [verified] (GDPR Article 27)
- Consent withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent (Article 7(3)), and withdrawal of Article 9 consent removes the processing basis for the health features entirely, so the product needs a defined degraded mode. [verified as to Article 7(3); the degraded-mode consequence is [inferred]]

### US state privacy laws (as of mid-2026)

- Roughly 20 comprehensive state privacy laws are in effect as of 2026. Precise geolocation is defined as sensitive personal data in nearly all of them; every state except California, Utah, and Iowa requires opt-in consent before processing sensitive data. California (CCPA/CPRA) instead gives consumers a "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information" right and opt-out of sale/sharing. [verified] (Consenteo, "The US State Privacy Law Tracker for 2026", https://www.consenteo.com/knowledge-hub/legal/us_state_privacy_law_tracker_2026, accessed 2026-07-30; Osano, "U.S. Data Privacy Laws: A Guide to the 2026 Landscape", https://www.osano.com/us-data-privacy-laws, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Maryland (MODPA, applying to processing from April 1, 2026) bans the sale of sensitive personal information outright, consent is irrelevant, and imposes the strictest data minimization standard in the US: collection must be reasonably necessary and proportionate to the specific product or service the consumer requested. Several states (Maryland, Oregon, Virginia as of July 1, 2026, Connecticut from October 1, 2026) prohibit the sale of precise geolocation data. [verified] (Consenteo tracker, above; Morgan Lewis, "US State Consumer Privacy Law Update", https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/07/us-state-consumer-privacy-law-update-notable-changes-across-existing-frameworks, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Texas TDPSA has no revenue or volume threshold, so it applies to Waypoint from day one of serving Texas users, with opt-in consent required for sensitive data including precise geolocation. Texas AG enforcement is active (2025 Allstate/Arity suit over geolocation harvested via mobile SDKs). [verified] (Promise Legal, "State Privacy Laws in 2026: Which Apply to Your App", https://blog.promise.legal/state-privacy-law-patchwork-2026/, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Twelve states require honoring Global Privacy Control (universal opt-out signals). [verified] (Consenteo tracker, above)

### FTC enforcement on location data

- The FTC treats precise location data as sensitive and enforces under FTC Act Section 5. Landmark orders: X-Mode/Outlogic (January 2024, first ban on selling sensitive location data), InMarket (January 2024, notice and consent failures for SDK-collected location), and Kochava (proposed settlement May 4, 2026, resolving the 2022 suit; bans sale of sensitive location data covering medical facilities, religious organizations, schools, shelters, and military installations without standalone affirmative express consent, and requires supplier consent audits and deletion pipelines). [verified] (FTC press releases: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/01/ftc-order-prohibits-data-broker-x-mode-social-outlogic-selling-sensitive-location-data and https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/ftc-ban-kochava-subsidiary-selling-sensitive-location-data-settle-charges-they-sold-location-data, accessed 2026-07-30; White & Case, "FTC settles with data broker Kochava", https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/ftc-settles-data-broker-kochava-over-sale-sensitive-location-data-key-takeaways, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Implication for Waypoint: never sell or share location data with ad tech or data brokers, obtain standalone consent for location collection, and audit any third-party SDK (analytics, crash reporting, maps) for location data leakage. The InMarket case shows liability attaches even when selling derived products (audience segments), not raw coordinates. [inferred from the orders above]

## 2. Health and fitness data

- GDPR Article 9: heart rate, training load, injury reports, and fitness metrics processed to assess fitness or health are "data concerning health", special category data. Processing is prohibited by default; for a consumer app the only realistic gateway is explicit consent under Article 9(2)(a), separate from terms acceptance, specific per purpose, and withdrawable. Step counts alone may be ordinary personal data, but they become health data when combined with heart rate or used to infer fitness levels, which is exactly Waypoint's use. [verified] (GDPR Article 9; GDPR Local, "GDPR for Wearable Technology", https://gdprlocal.com/gdpr-for-wearable-technology/, accessed 2026-07-30; Momentum, "GDPR Consent Requirements for Health Data", https://www.themomentum.ai/blog/gdpr-consent-requirements-health-data, accessed 2026-07-30)
- HIPAA does not apply: Waypoint is not a covered entity or business associate. This is a common misconception to correct in investor materials, and it does not mean the data is unregulated. [verified] (FTC, "Complying with FTC's Health Breach Notification Rule", https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-ftcs-health-breach-notification-rule-0, accessed 2026-07-30)
- FTC Health Breach Notification Rule (HBNR): the 2024 final rule (effective July 29, 2024) explicitly extended the rule to health and fitness apps by defining "covered health care provider" to include any online service that tracks fitness, vital signs, or bodily functions. A "breach" includes unauthorized disclosures (for example, leaking health data to an ad SDK), not just hacking. Notification to users, the FTC, and sometimes media is required; penalties run to roughly $50,000+ per violation. Waypoint is almost certainly a covered vendor of personal health records because it draws health data from multiple sources (HealthKit plus wearable APIs plus user input). [verified as to the rule; Waypoint's covered status is [inferred], high confidence] (FTC, "FTC Finalizes Changes to the Health Breach Notification Rule", https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-finalizes-changes-health-breach-notification-rule, accessed 2026-07-30; Wilson Sonsini analysis, https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/ftc-final-rule-officially-broadens-health-breach-notification-rule-targets-health-and-wellness-apps.html, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Washington My Health My Data Act (MHMD): applies to any business serving Washington consumers, no size threshold for the core duties, fully effective since June 30, 2024. "Consumer health data" expressly includes bodily functions, vital signs, measurements, biometric data, and inferences (RCW 19.373.010(8)), so heart rate and training load are squarely covered; precise location that could indicate seeking health services is also covered. [verified] (Washington AG, https://www.atg.wa.gov/protecting-washingtonians-personal-health-data-and-privacy, accessed 2026-07-30; RCW 19.373; Reuters Practical Law, https://www.reuters.com/practical-law-the-journal/transactional/understanding-washington-my-health-my-data-act-2024-04-01/, accessed 2026-07-30)
  - Obligations: a standalone Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy linked from the homepage (RCW 19.373.020); separate opt-in consent for collection beyond what is necessary for the requested service (RCW 19.373.030); separate consent for sharing; signed authorization for any sale (RCW 19.373.040); consumer rights to access, withdraw, and delete (RCW 19.373.050); security controls and restricted employee access (RCW 19.373.060); and written processor contracts (RCW 19.373.070). [verified] (D3rx compliance guide citing the RCW sections, https://d3rx.com/compliance-guides/washington-my-health-my-data-act, accessed 2026-07-30)
  - A geofencing prohibition bars virtual boundaries within 2,000 feet of in-person healthcare facilities used to track consumers or serve ads (RCW 19.373.080); route generation logic should avoid any healthcare-facility-aware targeting. [verified as to the prohibition; the route-generation implication is [inferred]]
  - Enforcement is by the Washington AG plus a private right of action via the Washington Consumer Protection Act, which makes MHMD a plaintiff-bar magnet and arguably the single riskiest US statute for Waypoint. [verified as to the private right of action; the risk ranking is [inferred]]
- Nevada SB 370 imposes similar consumer health data duties. [verified, noted for completeness] (covered in the Reuters and terms.law analyses above)
- EU EHDS (Regulation (EU) 2025/327, in force March 26, 2025): mostly phased to 2029 to 2031. Relevant to Waypoint only in two ways today: (a) a voluntary labelling and registration scheme applies if Waypoint claims interoperability with EHR systems (do not claim this at MVP), and (b) if Waypoint qualifies as a "health data holder", secondary-use data sharing duties arrive from March 26, 2029. Monitor, do not build for it now. [verified as to timeline and scheme; Waypoint's eventual data holder status is [assumption] A1] (European Commission, https://health.ec.europa.eu/ehealth-digital-health-and-care/european-health-data-space-regulation-ehds_en, accessed 2026-07-30; Regulation (EU) 2025/327 Recital 49 and Article 49, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327, accessed 2026-07-30)

## 3. Apple App Store rules (iOS-first, so these are gating)

- Guideline 5.1.3(i): data gathered in the health, fitness, and medical context (including HealthKit and Motion & Fitness APIs) may not be used or disclosed to third parties for advertising, marketing, or other use-based data mining. Selling HealthKit-derived data to data brokers or ad platforms is prohibited outright. Disclosure to a third party requires express user permission and the third party must itself provide a health or fitness service to the user. [verified] (Apple, App Review Guidelines section 5.1.3, https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/, accessed 2026-07-30; Apple, HealthKit "Protecting user privacy", https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Guideline 5.1.3(ii): apps must not write false data into HealthKit and may not store personal health information in iCloud. This constrains Waypoint's backend architecture (no CloudKit for health data). [verified] (same sources)
- Guideline 2.5.18 and 5.1.1: no targeted or behavioral advertising based on health or HealthKit data; consent required for any data collection with an accessible withdrawal path; purpose strings must fully describe use. [verified] (Apple guidelines, above)
- Privacy nutrition labels: App Store Connect submission requires declaring collection of health, fitness, and precise location data and whether it is linked to identity or used for tracking. Inaccurate labels are an enforcement and FTC deception risk. [verified] (Apple, "Health and fitness apps", https://developer.apple.com/health-fitness/, accessed 2026-07-30)
- App Tracking Transparency: if Waypoint never shares user data with other companies for cross-app tracking (the correct posture given 5.1.3), no ATT prompt is needed; adding any ad SDK or cross-app analytics would trigger ATT and conflict with the health data rules. [inferred from Apple's ATT policy and 5.1.3; design decision: stay tracking-free]
- From spring 2026 Apple lets Health & Fitness category apps declare medical device regulatory status per region; Waypoint should declare non-device wellness status. [verified as to the feature] (Apple, "Health and fitness apps", above)

## 4. AI-specific regulation

- EU AI Act status as of July 2026: the Digital Omnibus (Council final approval June 29, 2026) delayed high-risk (Annex III) obligations to December 2, 2027 and Annex I embedded systems to August 2, 2028, but did NOT delay Article 50 transparency obligations, which apply from August 2, 2026. GPAI model obligations have applied since August 2025 with Commission enforcement powers starting August 2, 2026. Fines for Article 50 breaches: up to EUR 15 million or 3 percent of worldwide turnover. Status caveat: the consolidated Omnibus text was awaiting Official Journal publication as of mid-July 2026; the new high-risk dates are near-certain but formally pending. [verified, with the pending-publication caveat stated] (European Commission, "Transparency obligations under Article 50 of the AI Act", https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act, accessed 2026-07-30; Stibbe, "The AI Act's Transparency Obligations", https://www.stibbe.com/publications-and-insights/the-ai-acts-transparency-obligations-rules-scope-and-timeline, accessed 2026-07-30; Astraea Counsel, https://astraea.law/insights/eu-ai-act-august-2026-us-companies, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Waypoint's classification: an AI running coach and route generator is not an Annex III high-risk use case (it does not gate access to essential services, employment, education, or credit). It is limited risk to the extent it interacts directly with users (conversational coaching), triggering the Article 50(1) duty to inform users they are interacting with AI, and minimal risk otherwise. Document this classification assessment in writing before EU launch. [inferred, high confidence, from Annex III text and Commission guidance] (European Commission, "Navigating the AI Act", https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/navigating-ai-act, accessed 2026-07-30; EU AI Act Article 6 and Annex III, https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/6/, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Caution on scope creep: if Waypoint ever triages injuries, assesses health risk for insurance, or makes binding health determinations, the classification changes (potentially Annex III access-to-healthcare, or medical device rules). Keep coaching advisory and non-diagnostic. [inferred]
- Liability and disclaimer norms for AI training advice. Category norms observed across current AI fitness products [verified as to the norms] (representative disclaimers: FlexFit, https://flexfit.ai/disclaimer, and FitCove, https://thefitcove.com/terms/, accessed 2026-07-30; Go Legal AI, UK health app disclaimer guide, https://go-legal.ai/health-app-legal-disclaimer-uk-free-template-compliance-guide/, accessed 2026-07-30):
  - A prominent "not medical advice, not a medical device, not FDA evaluated" statement.
  - An instruction to consult a physician before starting a program, with specific red-flag symptoms (chest pain, dizziness, diagnosed heart condition).
  - Express user assumption of the inherent risk of physical exercise.
  - Disclosure that AI-generated plans are not reviewed by a certified professional and cannot account for undisclosed conditions.
  - Liability caps and consequential damages exclusions to the extent local law permits.
  - Residual risk: disclaimers do not eliminate negligence liability, and EU/UK/Australian consumer law limits liability waivers, so the product itself must be defensively designed: conservative training load progression, heat and weather warnings, and route safety heuristics. [inferred]
- FDA: January 2026 guidance confirms low-risk general wellness products (software encouraging healthy activity, unrelated to diagnosis or treatment of disease) are not medical devices under FD&C Act section 520. Waypoint should stay inside the general wellness lane: no disease claims, no diagnosis, no treatment framing. [verified] (AHA News summarizing FDA guidance, https://www.aha.org/news/headline/2026-01-06-fda-issues-guidance-wellness-products-clinical-decision-support-software, accessed 2026-07-30)
- FTC AI marketing claims: Operation AI Comply (launched September 2024) continues under the current administration; every explicit and implicit claim about what Waypoint's AI does (personalization quality, safety, performance outcomes) must be substantiated with evidence before it is made. The December 2025 Rytr set-aside narrowed "means and instrumentalities" liability for tool makers but did not soften false-claims enforcement. [verified] (FTC, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes, accessed 2026-07-30; Benesch, "One Year In, FTC's Operation AI Comply Continues", https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/one-year-in-ftcs-operation-ai-comply-continues-under-new-administration-signaling-enduring-enforcement-focus/, accessed 2026-07-30)

## 5. Accessibility

- European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882), enforceable since June 28, 2025: it regulates services, not apps as such. A pure fitness tracker is arguably out of scope, but Waypoint's in-app subscription purchase flow is an e-commerce service (a service concluded at a distance via mobile app with a view to a consumer contract), which is squarely in scope. Practical consequence: at minimum the account, purchase, and subscription management flows must meet EN 301 549 (which incorporates WCAG 2.1 AA and adds native-app requirements: VoiceOver support, touch targets, gesture alternatives, respect for OS text size and contrast settings). Member state penalties reach EUR 100,000 in Germany. Microenterprise exemption (under 10 staff and under EUR 2 million turnover) may apply to service providers at MVP stage but is outgrown quickly. [verified as to the directive, standard, and e-commerce definition; the "purchase flow in scope, tracking features arguably out" reading is [inferred] from Directive Article 3(30) and Annex I] (EUR-Lex, Directive 2019/882, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj, accessed 2026-07-30; Auditsu, "Does the EAA Apply to Your Mobile App?", https://auditsu.com/resources/european-accessibility-act-mobile-apps, accessed 2026-07-30)
- US ADA Title III: no formal app accessibility standard for private businesses, but litigation is heavy and rising: 3,117 federal web/app accessibility suits in 2025 (up 27 percent), over 5,000 including state courts, with WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA the de facto benchmark courts and settlements reference. Mobile apps are named in a growing share of complaints. [verified] (Level Access litigation report, https://www.levelaccess.com/blog/2024-u-s-web-accessibility-litigation-key-trends-and-strategies-for-mitigating-risk/, accessed 2026-07-30; UsableNet 2025 year-end report, https://info.usablenet.com/hubfs/Remediated%20-%202025_Year-End_Digital_Accessibility_Lawsuit_Report_FINAL.pdf, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Practical target: build to WCAG 2.2 AA plus EN 301 549 Chapter 11 from the first sprint; audio cues during runs double as both an accessibility feature and a safety feature. [inferred recommendation]

## 6. Consumer subscription law (freemium model)

- FTC click-to-cancel (Negative Option Rule, 2024): vacated in its entirety by the Eighth Circuit on July 8, 2025 (Custom Communications, Inc. v. FTC) on procedural grounds, days before full enforcement. It is not in effect as of 2026-07-30. The FTC restarted rulemaking (ANPRM process began January to March 2026); a replacement rule is realistically years out. Status: unsettled, monitor. [verified] (Latham & Watkins, https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eighth-circuit-vacates-ftc-click-to-cancel-rule-days-before-compliance-deadline, accessed 2026-07-30; Mondaq, "FTC Moves To Revive Click-to-Cancel Rule", https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/consumer-law/1745320/, accessed 2026-07-30)
- But the floor never moved: ROSCA (15 U.S.C. 8401 et seq.) still requires clear disclosure of subscription terms, express informed consent before charging, and simple cancellation; FTC Act Section 5 still applies; and roughly 30 states (California, New York, Colorado, others) have their own auto-renewal laws, several of which already mandate online cancellation as easy as signup and renewal reminders. Build to the strictest state (California ARL) and the vacated federal rule as a spec. [verified as to ROSCA and the state law landscape; "build to the strictest" is a recommendation, [inferred]] (Promise Legal, "Subscription Billing Compliance: ROSCA & Click-to-Cancel", https://blog.promise.legal/startup-central/subscription-billing-compliance-rosca-ftc-click-to-cancel-dtc/, accessed 2026-07-30; Dickinson Wright, https://www.dickinson-wright.com/news-alerts/rule-interrupted-click-to-cancel-is-click-to-gone, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Mitigation: selling exclusively through Apple In-App Purchase delegates most billing, renewal notice, and cancellation mechanics to Apple's compliant flows, which materially reduces (not eliminates) auto-renewal law exposure. Marketing disclosures of price, term, and trial conversion remain Waypoint's duty. [inferred, high confidence]
- EU: Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU gives a 14-day withdrawal right for distance contracts; for digital services/content it is extinguished early only with the consumer's prior express consent plus acknowledgment of losing the right. From June 19, 2026, an EU-mandated withdrawal button must be provided in online interfaces (Germany transposed it as the Widerrufsbutton, alongside its existing paragraph 312k BGB cancellation button for terminating subscriptions, and German law caps post-term auto-renewal at indefinite renewal terminable monthly). Apple IAP handles some mechanics but Waypoint's own web funnel, if any, must implement these. [verified] (EUR-Lex, Directive 2011/83/EU, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A32011L0083, accessed 2026-07-30; Crowell & Moring, "EU Withdrawal Button Now Mandatory", https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/from-checkout-to-opt-out-the-eu-withdrawal-button-is-here-what-e-commerce-businesses-need-to-know, accessed 2026-07-30; Freshfields on the Widerrufsbutton, https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/pitfalls-for-e-commerce-how-the-new-eu-withdrawal-button-widerrufsbutton-wi-102ms91, accessed 2026-07-30)

## 7. MVP compliance checklist (build from day one)

1. Privacy zones by default: auto-generate an obfuscation zone around the user's home/start point; never render or share the true route origin; treat the start point as sensitive data end to end (Strava lesson). [inferred, industry standard]
   - Implementation note: obfuscation must be applied before any data leaves the device or reaches shared surfaces, not as a display-layer mask, or it repeats Strava's aggregation failure. [inferred]
2. Private by default: routes, activities, and stats are visible only to the user unless explicitly shared; no public heatmaps or proximity/flyby features at MVP. [inferred]
3. Granular, layered consent: separate opt-ins for (a) precise location, (b) health/fitness data (GDPR Article 9 explicit consent), (c) any sharing, each withdrawable in-app; never bundled into terms acceptance. [verified requirement under GDPR Article 9, MHMD, and 19 state laws]
   - Implementation note: record the affirmative statement, timestamp, and policy version per consent, since the EDPB expects explicit consent to be evidenced, and MHMD and state AGs will ask for proof. [verified as to the evidencing expectation] (sota.io, "Art. 9 Special Categories developer guide", https://sota.io/blog/gdpr-art-9-special-categories-personal-data-prohibition-exceptions-explicit-consent-health-biometric-developer-guide-2026, accessed 2026-07-30)
4. Standalone Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy linked prominently (Washington MHMD), plus the main privacy policy and accurate Apple privacy nutrition labels. [verified requirement]
5. Data minimization and retention schedule: collect only what route generation and coaching need; define retention per data type; default to on-device processing for raw GPS where feasible (also the Maryland MODPA necessity standard). [verified standard; on-device default is [inferred] best practice]
6. Full deletion pipeline: account deletion in-app (an Apple requirement), cascading to backups and processors, honoring GDPR Article 17, MHMD, and state law deletion rights within statutory windows. [verified requirement]
7. Data subject rights tooling: access/export (portability), correction, and Global Privacy Control recognition for the 12 GPC states. [verified requirement]
8. No ad tech, no data sales, no cross-app tracking: contractually and technically wall off health and location data from every third-party SDK; audit SDKs before integration (Apple 5.1.3, FTC location orders, Maryland sale ban). [verified requirement]
9. DPAs and processor inventory: Article 28 DPAs with cloud, maps, analytics, and AI model providers; SCCs or an adequacy mechanism for EU-to-US transfers; MHMD processor contracts. [verified requirement]
10. DPIA plus AI Act classification memo before EU launch; appoint EU and UK representatives if no local establishment. [verified requirement as to Articles 27 and 35; combined memo approach is [inferred]]
11. AI transparency: disclose clearly at first interaction that coaching is AI-generated (EU AI Act Article 50(1), applies August 2, 2026); label AI-generated plans in the UI. [verified requirement]
12.
