# Market Sizing: Digital Running Coaching (Hypothesis H5)

Version-Timestamp: 2026-07-30 14:05:00 UTC-4

**Executive summary.** This document tests H5: "The addressable market is venture-scale." Headline numbers: TAM for running app subscriptions globally is roughly $1.3B to $2.9B in 2025 per published market research (low-credibility report mills), cross-checked against observable company revenue of roughly $0.6B to $1.0B, growing 11% to 14% per year, inside a broader fitness app market of $6B to $12B. SAM (committed amateur runners on iOS in the US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia) is approximately 14 million people, a theoretical spend ceiling of roughly $1.4B per year at Runna-level pricing, of which demonstrated current willingness to pay supports roughly $150M to $300M. SOM at 3 to 5 years, benchmarked against what Runna actually achieved as the breakout specialist app of this cycle, is 70,000 to 300,000 paying subscribers, or roughly $5M to $30M ARR (about 1% to 2% of SAM). Verdict: H5 is UNCERTAIN, leaning supported only with an expansion story. The category is real, growing, and has produced two acquisitions and one near-IPO, but the route-generation running coach wedge alone points to a strong $10M to $30M ARR business, not a venture-scale outcome, unless Waypoint credibly expands beyond the initial wedge (Android, more geographies, broader coaching or outdoor navigation surface).

## 1. Scope and method

- Market definition used throughout: consumer subscription revenue for mobile apps whose primary job is running training, coaching, tracking, or route planning. Excludes hardware (watches), gym memberships, human coaching, and race fees.
- Geographies for SAM: US, UK, EU-27, Canada, Australia (iOS-first, English-first launch plan; EU included because running participation and subscription spend are high even where English is a second language).
- Every claim is tagged [verified] (directly sourced), [inferred] (derived from verified inputs, arithmetic shown), or [assumption] (judgment call, listed again in Section 8).
- All sources accessed 2026-07-30.

## 2. TAM: top-down

### 2.1 Published running app market figures (report mills, low credibility)

Three syndicated research firms publish a "Running Apps Market" number. All are low-credibility report mills: methodology is not disclosed beyond segment lists, and the figures disagree by more than 2x. They are included because they are the only published top-down numbers for this exact category, and their growth rates cluster usefully.

| Source | 2025 value | Forecast | CAGR | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SkyQuest, "Running Apps Market" (report SQMIG45E2759, published April 2026, global, all platforms and monetization types). https://www.skyquestt.com/report/running-apps-market | $1.28B | $3.7B by 2033 | 14.1% | [verified as published; credibility low] |
| Dataintelo, "Running Apps Market Research Report 2034" (global, base year 2025). https://dataintelo.com/report/global-running-apps-market | $1.8B | $4.6B by 2034 | 10.9% | [verified as published; credibility low] |
| WiseGuyReports, "Running App Market" (global, base year 2025). https://www.wiseguyreports.com/reports/running-app-market | $2.88B | $8.5B by 2035 | 11.5% | [verified as published; credibility low] |

Consensus reading: running apps globally generate roughly $1.3B to $2.9B in 2025 and grow 11% to 14% per year [inferred from the three reports above].

### 2.2 Higher-credibility cross-checks

- Health and fitness apps overall generated close to $6.0B in 2025 (up 17.7% year over year), with about 80% of that from subscriptions. Source: Business of Apps, "Health & Fitness App Report 2026", https://www.businessofapps.com/data/health-fitness-app-report/, corroborated by eMarketer, "The health and fitness industry's future is subscription-first", https://www.emarketer.com/content/health-fitness-industry-s-future-subscription-first-not-hardware. [verified]
- Sensor Tower measured global health and fitness in-app purchase revenue on track to exceed $4B in 2025, with the US taking more than half of spend and the UK about 8%. Source: Sensor Tower, "State of Mobile Health & Fitness Apps 2025", https://sensortower.com/blog/state-of-mobile-health-and-fitness-in-2025. [verified] (Sensor Tower measures app store IAP only, so it understates web-billed subscriptions; Strava itself said Sensor Tower "significantly underestimates" its revenue, per Sacra, https://sacra.com/c/strava/.)
- Grand View Research puts the broader fitness apps market at $12.12B in 2025 growing 13.4% to $33.58B by 2033 (global, includes all fitness app types and ad revenue). Source: https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/fitness-app-market. [verified as published; mid credibility, broad definition]
- Bottom-up cross-check on the running-specific number: Strava alone reported roughly $415M to $500M revenue in 2025 (Business of Apps says $415M, https://www.businessofapps.com/data/strava-statistics/; Sacra and IPO coverage say ARR approaching $500M, https://sacra.com/c/strava/). Runna, Komoot (route planning, 2024 revenue about EUR 50M per M&A reporting, see Section 4), Nike Run Club (free), Garmin Connect (mostly free), plus smaller training apps plausibly add a few hundred million more. Observable running-adjacent app revenue is therefore roughly $0.6B to $1.0B today [inferred], which sits below the report-mill midpoint and suggests the true running-only TAM is at the low end of the published range.

### 2.3 TAM statement and limitations

TAM: roughly $1.5B in 2025 for running app subscriptions globally, growing 11% to 14% per year toward $3B to $5B by the early 2030s [inferred from 2.1 and 2.2, weighting the observable-revenue cross-check over the report mills].

Limitations: (a) the only category-exact figures come from report mills with undisclosed methodology; (b) definitions blur running vs general fitness vs outdoor navigation; (c) subscription spend is concentrated (US plus UK is roughly 60% of category spend per Sensor Tower), so global TAM overstates what an English-first app can reach; (d) a large share of current TAM is already captured by Strava.

### 3.2 Narrowing to the target user

- Committed amateurs (3 to 5 runs per week): assume 25% of participants, giving ~27.5M across the five geographies [assumption]. Directional support: SFIA distinguishes "core" from casual participants, and OIA explicitly notes 2025 growth came from "casual, lower-frequency runners" while "the dedicated core has softened" (OIA 2026 Running Report, above) [verified context]. The 25% figure itself is a judgment call.
- iOS share by geography (WorldPopulationReview compilation of Statcounter data, 2025, https://worldpopulationreview.com/country-rankings/iphone-market-share-by-country): US 58.9%, Canada 59.9%, Australia 58.0%, UK ~49% to 51% (Statcounter UK, https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/united-kingdom), Germany 37.6% used as EU proxy. [verified]
- Weighted iOS share across the runner base: (52.3M x 0.589) + (8.0M x 0.50) + (43M x 0.38) + (4M x 0.60) + (3M x 0.58) = 30.8 + 4.0 + 16.3 + 2.4 + 1.7 = 55.2M of 110.3M, about 50% [inferred].
- SAM population: 27.5M committed amateurs x ~50% iOS = ~14M people [inferred].

### 3.3 SAM in dollars

- Theoretical ceiling: 14M x $100 per year (between Runna's $119.99 annual and Strava's $79.99 annual, see Section 5) = ~$1.4B per year [inferred].
- Demonstrated willingness to pay is far lower. RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2025 (75,000+ apps, $10B+ revenue analyzed, https://www.revenuecat.com/pdf/state-of-subscription-apps-2025.pdf) puts median download-to-paid conversion for health and fitness at 2.7%, top decile 12.1% [verified]. Among committed runners (already self-selected as engaged), assume 10% to 15% currently pay for at least one running subscription [assumption, anchored on: Strava's paying base vs its registered base is single-digit percent, but committed runners are the segment that does pay].
- Realistic serviceable spend pool: 14M x 10% to 15% x $100 = roughly $140M to $210M per year today, call it $150M to $300M allowing ARPU upside [inferred]. This is the pool Waypoint actually competes for in years 1 to 5.

## 4. SOM: what comparable specialist apps actually achieved

The anchor set, all traction numbers as reported:

- **Runna** (closest comparable: paid running coaching app, UK, founded 2021, launched March 2022). By April 2025 (year 3): approximately 90,000 paying members and nearly 150 staff, having raised £8M; acquired by Strava April 17, 2025, price undisclosed, with reporting of up to 30x returns for early investors. Sources: Tech Funding News, https://techfundingnews.com/strava-acquires-runna-5-key-takeaways-and-how-it-stacks-up-against-nike-run-club-myfitnesspal-and-fitbit/ [verified as reported; the 90,000 figure is single-sourced]; Strava press release, https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-to-acquire-runna-a-leading-running-training-app [verified]. Note the conflict: Strava's press release says Runna "helped millions of runners", which refers to total users and trials, not payers. I weight the TFN 90,000 paying members figure because it is specific and consistent with headcount and funding stage. Implied ARR at exit: 90,000 x ~$110 blended = ~$10M [inferred].
- **Strava** (category ceiling). 180M registered users end of 2025, revenue $415M (Business of Apps) to ~$500M ARR (Sacra, and IPO coverage citing ~$2.2B valuation and a confidential IPO filing in early 2026), 90% from subscriptions, after 16 years. Sources: https://www.businessofapps.com/data/strava-statistics/, https://sacra.com/c/strava/, https://techicons.com/latest-news/strava-ipo-filing-500-million-revenue/. [verified as reported; exact paid subscriber count is not disclosed]
- **Komoot** (route-planning adjacent). 45M registered users after 17 years; 2024 revenue reportedly EUR 50M with EUR 12M EBITDA; acquired by Bending Spoons March 2025, price undisclosed. Sources: Business Wire, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250320568940/en/Bending-Spoons-Acquires-komoot [verified]; revenue per M&A intelligence reporting (mainsights.io citing NKP), https://www.mainsights.io/ma-news/italian-software-company-bending-spoons-acquires-majority-stake-in-german-tech-firm-komoot-a-hikingbiking-trip-planner-application [verified as reported, unconfirmed by company].
- **AllTrails** (adjacent success story: outdoor route discovery). 1M paid subscribers in January 2021 after ~11 years (PRNewswire, https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/alltrails-celebrates-1-million-paid-subscribers-301214556.html) [verified]; profitable since 2017 (TechCrunch, https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/17/alltrails-raises-150m-after-covid-accelerates-peoples-interest-in-exploring-the-outdoors/) [verified]; 95M+ registered users by March 2026 (TIME100, https://time.com/collection/time100-most-influential-companies/2026/alltrails/) [verified]. A FY2025 revenue figure of $210M and ~1.5M paid subscribers circulates only on a low-credibility analysis site (businessmodelcanvastemplate.com) and should be treated as unverified [assumption if used].
- **TrainAsONE** (AI running coach, cautionary tale). Free basic tier with premium around $10 per month; described in 2026 competitor comparisons as having a dated interface; no user or revenue numbers found anywhere credible. Source: https://nxtrun.ai/best-ai-running-app (a competitor's comparison page, low credibility) [verified as reported]. The absence of traction reporting after roughly a decade is itself the data point: AI running coaching without distribution or brand has not produced a visible business.

SOM statement: the realistic obtainable outcome for a well-executed specialist running app at years 3 to 5 is bounded below by TrainAsONE (approximately zero visible traction) and anchored by Runna (~90,000 payers at year 3 as the breakout winner with strong paid marketing and App Store featuring). Waypoint, with a differentiated route-generation wedge, targeting 70,000 to 300,000 paying subscribers by year 5 is defensible; above that requires Runna-beating execution. At $70 to $100 net ARPU (after App Store commission of 15% to 30% on the $119.99 gross), that is roughly **$5M to $30M ARR, about 1% to 2% of SAM** [inferred].

## 5. Bottom-up sanity check

Verified price points (all accessed 2026-07-30):

- Runna: $19.99 per month or $119.99 per year; Strava+Runna bundle $149.99 per year. Source: https://www.runna.com/pricing [verified].
- Strava: $11.99 per month or $79.99 per year (US). Source: Runify citing current pricing, https://www.runifyapp.com/blog/strava-usage-statistics, consistent with Strava subscription pages [verified].
- AllTrails: Plus $35.99 per year; Peak (AI route features) $79.99 per year, launched June 2025. Source: AllTrails press, https://www.alltrails.com/press/alltrails-expands-membership-offering-with-alltrails-peak, and USA Today, https://www.usatoday.com/story/shopping/2025/06/11/alltrails-peak-membership/84136256007/ [verified].
- Komoot: Premium about EUR 5 per month per heise, https://www.heise.de/en/news/Hiking-app-Komoot-is-sold-10324361.html [verified].

Bottom-up model, year 5, inputs tagged:

1. Committed amateur runners, five geographies: 27.5M [inferred, Section 3.2].
2. Share willing to pay for a running app subscription: 10% to 15% [assumption] gives 2.75M to 4.1M payers.
3. iOS share of those payers: ~50% [verified inputs, inferred weighting] gives 1.4M to 2.1M addressable payers.
4. Waypoint capture of addressable payers by year 5: 5% to 15% [assumption, Runna-anchored: Runna took roughly 5% of this pool in 3 years] gives 70,000 to 310,000 subscribers.
5. Blended gross ARPU: $90 to $110 per year (annual-plan heavy, priced between Strava and Runna) [assumption anchored on verified pricing]; net of platform commission: $70 to $95.
6. Bottom-up SOM revenue: 70,000 x $70 = ~$5M to 310,000 x $95 = ~$29M ARR [inferred].

Comparison against top-down: 1% to 2% of the ~$1.4B SAM ceiling is $14M to $28M, matching the bottom-up range. The two methods agree at the SOM level.

Where top-down and bottom-up disagree: at the TAM level. The report-mill figures ($1.8B to $2.9B for running apps in 2025) imply substantially more spend than is visible in company-reported and estimated revenues (Strava $415M to $500M, Komoot ~EUR 50M, Runna ~$10M at acquisition plus post-acquisition growth, AllTrails partially running-relevant, long tail small). Observable spend supports a running-app TAM nearer $0.6B to $1.0B today. I weight the observable-revenue view and treat the published TAM as an upper bound, because the report mills do not disclose methodology and their spread (2.25x between low and high) signals weak underlying data.

## 6. Verdict on H5

**H5 is UNCERTAIN: the wedge market is real but the wedge alone is not venture-scale; the venture story requires credible expansion, and the exit environment partially rescues it.**

Supporting evidence: the category monetizes well (health and fitness apps lead all categories in payer LTV per RevenueCat 2025), it is growing double digits (11% to 17% across independent sources), Strava reached ~$500M ARR and filed for IPO at $2.2B, and two strategic acquirers (Strava, Bending Spoons) bought specialist route or training apps in 2025 alone. AllTrails Peak shipping AI route generation at $79.99 per year in 2025 validates paid demand for exactly Waypoint's hero feature.

Refuting evidence: the honest 3-to-5-year revenue outcome for a specialist app is $5M to $30M ARR, which supports a healthy company and a Runna-style strategic exit (likely tens of millions, price undisclosed) but not, on its own, a standard venture story that needs a credible path to $100M+ ARR. Strava's free tier plus Runna plus AllTrails Peak means the incumbent set already bundles most of Waypoint's surface.

What would change the answer: (a) Runna's post-acquisition subscriber trajectory (if it passed 500,000+ payers by 2026, the ceiling for specialists is higher than modeled); (b) direct evidence that route generation specifically, not training plans, drives willingness to pay (AllTrails Peak attach rates would be the best proxy); (c) validated Android and non-English expansion economics, which roughly double SAM; (d) evidence that the "share willing to pay" among committed runners exceeds 15% (for example from Waypoint's own landing-page or paywall tests).

## 7. Assumptions (collected)

1. 25% of runners in launch geographies are committed amateurs (3 to 5 runs per week).
2. EU-27 adult running participation rate of 12%; Canada 13%; Australia 15%.
3. 10% to 15% of committed runners currently pay, or would pay, for at least one running app subscription.
4. Waypoint can capture 5% to 15% of addressable iOS payers by year 5 (Runna-anchored).
5. Blended gross ARPU of $90 to $110 per year; platform commission 15% to 30%.
6. AllTrails FY2025 revenue of $210M and ~1.5M subscribers (single low-credibility source; used only directionally).
7. Report-mill TAM figures overstate true running app spend; observable company revenue is the better anchor.

## 8. Open questions

1. Runna's paying subscriber count today (mid 2026), post Strava acquisition and bundle launch. This is the single most valuable missing number for SOM.
2. Strava's actual paid subscriber count (never disclosed; the IPO S-1, when public, will settle it and sharpen ARPU and conversion benchmarks).
3. AllTrails Peak adoption: what share of AllTrails payers chose the AI route-generation tier over Plus.
4. Pan-EU running participation from a single credible survey (Eurobarometer sport data would improve the EU estimate).
5. Whether "committed amateur" willingness to pay differs materially for route generation vs training plans (primary research needed; this is H-level validation work, not desk research).
6. Komoot paying subscriber count and revenue mix under Bending Spoons (would sharpen the route-planning monetization benchmark).
