# Market Landscape: Running Apps, Fitness Coaching, and the AI Coaching Category

Version-Timestamp: 2026-07-30 14:05:00 UTC-4

Waypoint enters a running software market that is small in dollar terms (published estimates for running apps cluster around 1 to 2 billion USD globally in 2025 to 2026) but sits inside a much larger and faster growing fitness app market (roughly 12 to 14 billion USD) and rides on top of a genuine, measurable running participation boom: 52.3 million Americans ran on roads in 2025, England added over 500,000 runners in a single year to reach 7.1 million, race participation has exceeded pre-pandemic levels with historically low event churn, and Strava now claims over 180 million registered users with running as its top sport. The competitive structure changed materially in 2025: Strava acquired Runna (the leading AI training plan app) and reached a 2.2 billion USD valuation, Bending Spoons acquired Komoot (the leading route planning app, 45 million users), and both Garmin (Connect+, March 2025) and Apple (Workout Buddy, watchOS 26) shipped paid or platform-level AI coaching. The strategically important observation for Waypoint is that route planning apps and training apps remain two separate product categories with separate owners: nobody currently answers "where should I run, right now, from here, for me" as a first-class product, and the two assets that could combine to do so (Runna and Komoot) landed in different acquirers' portfolios in the same year.

[verified] (comparison of MarkWide Research, Dataintelo, Business Research Insights, and Econ Market Research running app reports, all accessed 2026-07-30; URLs in section 2.2)

### 1.2 Route planning versus training: two categories, not one

Route planning and navigation apps (Komoot, Strava routes, RunGo, Footpath) answer "where do I go"; training apps (Runna, TrainAsONE, Coopah, VDOT O2) answer "what workout do I do." The market treats these as distinct categories with distinct owners:

- Komoot is positioned as "Europe's leading outdoor platform" for route planning and navigation across hiking, cycling, and running, with 45 million users at acquisition in March 2025. It is outdoor-adventure-first, not run-training-first. [verified] (Business Wire, "Bending Spoons Acquires komoot", 2025-03-20, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250320568940/en/Bending-Spoons-Acquires-komoot, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Strava's route features (heatmap-based route discovery and planning) are a premium subscription feature inside a social tracking platform, not a standalone generation product. [verified] (Sacra, "Strava revenue, funding & growth rate", https://sacra.com/c/strava/, accessed 2026-07-30, which lists route planning among premium features)
- Runna generates personalized training plans (workout content, paces, progression) from user inputs about experience, goals, and schedule; it does not generate routes. [verified] (Tech Funding News, "Strava acquires Runna: 5 key takeaways", 2025-04, https://techfundingnews.com/strava-acquires-runna-5-key-takeaways-and-how-it-stacks-up-against-nike-run-club-myfitnesspal-and-fitbit/, accessed 2026-07-30)
- TrainAsONE markets AI-adaptive run training (it appears in analyst competitor lists for the AI coaching running app category) and similarly prescribes workouts, not routes. [verified] (Dataintelo, "AI Coaching Running App Market Research Report 2034", https://dataintelo.com/report/ai-coaching-running-app-market, accessed 2026-07-30, key companies list)

[inferred] Waypoint's hero feature (constraint-aware, personalized, on-demand route generation with coaching layered on top) sits at the intersection of these two categories rather than inside either one. Reasoning: the verified product descriptions above show training apps prescribe the workout but leave route selection to the user, while routing apps plan static routes without knowledge of the user's training state, and the 2025 acquisitions placed the leading training asset (Runna, under Strava) and the leading routing asset (Komoot, under Bending Spoons) in different owners' hands, making a first-party fusion of the two less likely in the near term.

### 3.2 How value flows

[verified, composite of sources already cited] The dominant consumer monetization is freemium subscription: Strava (79.99 USD/yr), Runna (119.99 USD/yr), Komoot Premium (59.99 EUR/yr), Garmin Connect+ (69.99 USD/yr), against large free anchors (Nike Run Club, base Garmin Connect, base Strava). Hardware makers capture the largest absolute dollars (Garmin fitness segment alone, 2.36B USD in 2025, exceeds every published estimate of the entire running app software market), then monetize attached subscriptions. Data flows from wearables (Apple Watch is the most popular device used with Strava) into platform hubs (Apple Health, Garmin Connect, Strava), and Strava's API is the de facto interchange for 100+ specialist apps. (Device popularity: Business of Apps, URL above, accessed 2026-07-30.)

[inferred] Implication: a new specialist app does not need to win the wearable layer; it needs read access to it (HealthKit, Garmin API, Strava API) and a differentiated job the hubs do not do. The 2025 pattern (Strava buying its best API developer, Runna) shows the hub will acquire specialist apps that reach scale on its rails, which is both an exit path and a platform risk.

### 3.3 Distribution economics (App Store, Play Store)

- Apple's standard commission is 30% on paid apps and in-app purchases; the Small Business Program (since January 2021) reduces it to 15% for developers under 1M USD in prior-year proceeds; subscriptions retained beyond 12 months drop to 15% for all developers. [verified] (Apple Developer, "App Store Small Business Program", https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/, accessed 2026-07-30)
- Since the April 2025 ruling in Epic v. Apple (Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers), US developers can link to external payment methods without Apple commission or warning screens; the matter remained under further review as of mid-2026. In the EU, DMA-driven alternative terms include reduced rates. [verified] (TMS Outsource, "Apple App Store Fees for Developers", https://tms-outsource.com/blog/posts/apple-app-store-fees/, accessed 2026-07-30; secondary source, cross-checked against the Apple Developer page above for program mechanics)
- Web checkout materially improves specialist app economics: Runna's web subscription channel (via Paddle as merchant of record) delivered 15% higher retention than in-app subscribers, alongside avoided store commissions. [verified] (Paddle case study, URL above, accessed 2026-07-30)

### 3.4 Where a route-generation-first product sits

[inferred] Synthesis of sections 3.1 to 3.3: Waypoint would enter as a specialist app on top of the wearable and hub layers, in the white space between routing (Komoot: outdoor, static, plan-ahead) and training (Runna: workout prescription without location intelligence). Its natural distribution is iOS App Store plus web checkout, its natural data dependencies are HealthKit, weather APIs, and map/street data, and its natural integration surface is the Strava API (which is simultaneously the likeliest acquirer channel and the likeliest fast-follow threat, since Strava owns both the social graph and, post-Runna, the training layer). Route generation quality (constraint satisfaction: distance, elevation, crossings, surface, safety, weather) is the defensible technical core because none of the incumbents' verified product descriptions include it.

[inferred] Read-through for Waypoint's target user (committed amateur, 3 to 5 runs/week, races occasionally): every verified series points the same direction. The base of runners is growing in the US and England, the growth skews toward beginners and Gen Z racers who buy training help (Runna's own base is 60% beginner or intermediate), race demand exceeds pre-pandemic levels, and racers churn between events rather than repeating them, which sustains recurring demand for goal-based coaching. The OIA's caveat that the "dedicated core has softened" in the US is the one countervailing signal and directly concerns Waypoint's hypothesized core user; it should be stress-tested in the persona research.

## Assumptions (all must be validated)

1. MarkWide's 612M USD running app figure uses a narrower, running-only definition than the other three vendors (their public pages do not fully disclose definitions).
2. The scale of secondary AI training apps (Coopah, VDOT O2, Humango, Athletica) and running navigation apps (RunGo, Footpath) is small relative to Runna and Komoot; no public figures were found.
3. The 621 million global runners figure circulating on aggregator sites is unreliable and should not be used.
4. No credible pan-EU running participation figure was sourced in this pass; Eurobarometer or national surveys must be pulled for the sizing document.
5. Widely reported record demand at major marathons (for example London Marathon record finishers and ballot entries in 2025 to 2026) was not verified against a primary source in this pass and is excluded from the findings above until sourced.

## Open questions

1. What are Runna's and Komoot's actual subscriber counts (not MAU or registered users)? Both owners are private and disclose selectively; Sensor Tower estimates exist but were disputed in Strava's case.
2. Will Strava fold route generation into Runna (or Athlete Intelligence), collapsing Waypoint's white space? Monitoring Strava product announcements is a standing task.
3. How does Apple's Workout Buddy roadmap evolve (it currently coaches during workouts but does not plan training or routes), and does Apple Intelligence hardware gating (iPhone 15 Pro+) meaningfully limit its reach in Waypoint's launch window?
4. What is the post-acquisition trajectory of Komoot under Bending Spoons (aggressive monetization plus 75 to 85% staff cuts): does it degrade the incumbent routing experience and enlarge the opening for a running-first routing product?
5. Which market definition will investors accept for the sizing document: running apps (about 1 to 2B USD), digital coaching (about 5B USD), or fitness apps (about 12 to 14B USD)? The layer choice changes the TAM narrative materially.
6. Is the softening of the "dedicated core" US runner (OIA 2026) a measurement artifact or a real behavioral shift away from Waypoint's target user?
