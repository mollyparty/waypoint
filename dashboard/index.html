Waypoint at a glance

The running app that knows where you should run.

The concept in one breath: every training app prescribes workouts without knowing where you are standing; every route app draws static lines without knowing what your body needs today. Waypoint answers the question both halves ignore: "where should I run, right now, from here, for me." Constraint-based route generation is the hero feature; an adaptive coaching layer connects your training state to where you actually go; voice navigation executes it; privacy is day-one architecture.

What matters most

  • Participation is booming, led by Gen Z: global runners grew from 672M to 785M (2022 to 2025); record London Marathon ballot (1.13M applications); Strava run clubs quadrupled.
  • The enabling stack is nearly free: OpenStreetMap routing, free elevation data, WeatherKit's free tier, Apple's on-device AI. The moat must be personalization and proprietary context data, not routing algorithms.
  • 2025 consolidated the map: Strava bought Runna; Bending Spoons bought Komoot and cut ~85 percent of staff. The two assets that could fuse training and routing landed with different owners.
  • Platform squeeze: Garmin Connect+ ($6.99/mo) and Apple Workout Buddy commoditize generic AI coaching chat. Differentiation must be what they do not do.

Compliance guardrails (day-one architecture)

  • Location is the most sensitive surface: routes anchored at home reveal home addresses (the Strava heatmap precedent). Privacy zones and private-by-default are mandatory from the MVP.
  • Heart rate and training load are GDPR Article 9 special category data; the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and Washington MHMD apply to consumer fitness apps.
  • EU AI Act Article 50 (disclose users are talking to AI) applies from August 2, 2026.
  • A 15-item MVP compliance checklist is ready in regulatory-compliance.md.

Phase 4 · Synthesis

Four documents: opportunity, positioning, hypothesis review, and the concept proposed for lock. Full detail in research/04-synthesis/.

The opportunity in three sentences

Every training app prescribes workouts without knowing where the runner is standing, and every route app plans static lines without knowing what the runner's body needs today. Waypoint claims that empty intersection with constraint-based adaptive route generation as the acquisition wedge, activated by the travel moment and retained daily by safety-aware routing and route novelty, with the coaching layer as the paid tier and the defensible seam. The enabling stack costs near zero at the margin, the one open personalization pathway (HealthKit) matches the iOS-first plan, and the window before the only credible incumbent assembles the same capability is estimated at 12 to 18 months.

The feature architecture

Free tier anchor

On-demand route generation with constraints: distance, elevation, surface, crossing minimization, safety-aware and weather-aware routing. Route novelty ("roads you have not run"). Travel mode.

Paid adaptive layer

Training-state-aware generation (today's workout shapes the route). Adaptive routines that survive real life, calibrated between Runna-too-hard and TrainAsONE-too-soft. A coach that explains its reasoning.

Execution surface

Voice-guided turn-by-turn so the phone stays pocketed. Apple Watch companion. Post-run share to Strava (never compete with the social graph).

The honest risk ledger (what an investor must believe)

  • H5 is the weak link: the wedge alone is a $5M to $30M ARR business. The venture story requires the expansion path: iOS English-first, then Android, more geographies, then the same route engine pointed at walking, cycling, and hiking.
  • The Strava clock is real: speed is a business requirement, not a preference.
  • The safety tightrope: safety-aware routing is the strongest evidence AND the biggest liability. Never market "safe routes" as a guarantee.
  • Validation debt: everything so far is desk research. The interview backlog runs alongside Phase 5.

The journeys that define the experience

  • Onboarding: install to first generated route in under 3 minutes; permissions asked at the moment of value, never at cold launch; location denial degrades to a manual start point, never a dead end.
  • Marcus's daily run: boredom to a route with never-run streets matched to today's workout; the paid upsell fires once, contextually, and the free path is never degraded to force it.
  • Priya's hotel lobby: travel context auto-activates away from home; a route in under 30 seconds with a "why this route" card of checkable reasons; night-before pre-generation for offline mornings.
  • Elena's dark morning: safety-aware weighting as a first-class constraint; the copy rule is enforceable ("safety-aware" is the ceiling, "safe" fails review); missing data degrades honestly, including the honest refusal.
  • Jake's missed week: no streak shame, a one-question triage, a rebuilt plan with reasoning shown, and a comeback run built to be finished.

Ten design principles govern all flows, led by: explain reasoning always, degrade honestly, never promise "safe", ask at the moment of value, the free path always works, feed Strava and never compete with it.

Claudio's instruction: infrastructure detail must not block product definition, so DEC-009 names four checkpoints where this can be re-opened without ceremony (before the walking skeleton, when architecture design starts, at Aiven contract time, or if two databases plus self-owned auth proves to be real ops burden).

Round 2 changed the answer. The security-first database deep dive (database-deep-dive.md) asked your exact question, "is Supabase really necessary," and the honest answer is no: an option the earlier rounds never put on the table beats it on every one of your four pillars. The recommended data layer is now a split architecture:

  • Personal data (accounts, entitlements, privacy zones): Aiven for PostgreSQL, an EU-incorporated (Finland) specialist with ISO 27001 plus SOC 2 Type II, dedicated VMs, a 99.99 percent credit-backed SLA, and point-in-time recovery standard.   • Auth: decoupled into Waypoint's own API layer (Better Auth), with identity tables living in Aiven. This deletes round 1's hardest exit problem (auth lock-in) instead of merely containing it. Runner-up overall: OVHcloud managed Postgres.

Round 1 (stack-validation.md) had confirmed Supabase "with conditions"; round 2 supersedes that verdict because it compared against the EU-owned managed-Postgres specialists round 1 skipped, and because splitting the rebuildable moat store from the small precious personal-data store shrinks both the compliance surface and the blast radius. Unchanged from round 1: Postgres+PostGIS itself, GraphHopper on Hetzner, and the on-device health-data wall.

Phase 6 · Business model and go-to-market

Five documents: business model canvas, revenue model, unit economics, metrics, and the go-to-market plan. Full detail in research/06-business-model/. Six gate decisions sit at the bottom of this section.

The one sentence that matters most in this phase: Waypoint's unit economics are a function of its distribution strategy, not its pricing. Paid acquisition returns 53 to 70 cents on the dollar at every price in the credible band, so the business clears a healthy lifetime-value-to-acquisition-cost ratio only if roughly 84 percent or more of subscribers arrive organically. The community-first go-to-market plan is therefore load-bearing arithmetic, not a bootstrapping preference. If it fails, no price rescues the model.

The tactical rule worth remembering: list high and discount, never the reverse. Go-to-market

Beachhead: the urban committed amateur who runs three to five times a week from a home doorstep, in one metro, with women runners as the highest-intensity cohort. Travelers are the demo carriers, not the target. The geographic concentration is structural rather than stylistic: route quality depends on OpenStreetMap pedestrian data density, crossings are the hardest features to map, and a thin national launch would ship bad routes to most of the country.

Channels, ranked: physical run clubs first (new Strava clubs nearly quadrupled in 2025, and the channel solves the demo problem, since the value is invisible in a screenshot and obvious in ninety seconds standing next to someone), then Reddit and running forums, then store optimization as mandatory infrastructure. Two popular channels are argued against explicitly: SEO content, structurally impaired by AI Overviews cutting clicks 39.8 to 58 percent, and paid acquisition, where a bought install before month 10 has a lifetime value of exactly zero and contaminates the retention signal.

Launch shape: iOS at month 9 to 10 is a quiet quality burn-in; the Android date is the real public launch, because the one-shot assets can only be spent once and a mixed-platform run club is the wrong room in which to tell half the people "not yet". Hard fallback: if Android is not certified by month 13, launch on iOS alone within two weeks.

Metrics

North Star: Weekly Routed Runners, the count of distinct runners completing two or more generated routes in a rolling seven days. The 80 percent completion floor sits inside the definition, so the metric mechanically cannot rise by shipping more routes people abandon. Rejected alternatives and why: raw monthly actives (inflatable by the Android launch, hides a collapsing weekly cadence for a month), total routes generated (disqualifying, because it rises when routes are bad, since rejection drives regeneration), and total distance run (a cohort using Waypoint as a plain GPS tracker would score perfectly while refuting the core hypothesis).

Activation: first generated route completed as a recorded run within seven days of install. The PRD's current "generate and start" definition counts mid-run abandonment as success, which is exactly the bad-route signature the MVP most needs to see.

Route quality gets eleven behavioral signals collapsed into one weekly Good Route Rate, because users will rarely rate anything explicitly. The strongest positive proxy is the 30-day repeat rate; the quietest and most damning negative is the reveal abandon rate. The most important guardrail is honest-refusal stability: the easiest way to lift acceptance is to quietly relax constraints when the engine struggles, and a product whose honest refusals disappear looks like a product that got better.

Cohort retention is the number the whole MVP exists to produce and the gate for the paid layer, and it needs a stable per-user identifier over time. So the privacy-preserving analytics choice cannot deliver the metric the product's own goals require.

Keep cohort retention in first-party PostgresRecommended. Keyed on the account identifier already held for sync: no new vendor, no new consent basis, a handful of hand-built SQL views. TelemetryDeck stays for aggregate product analytics.
Add PostHog EUMore capable out of the box, but it is a new vendor, a new consent basis, and a new data-processing agreement. Better as a deliberate v1.x decision than a default.

4 · Re-base two PRD targets

Awaiting decision
