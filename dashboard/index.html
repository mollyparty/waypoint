Waypoint at a glance

The running app that knows where you should run.

The concept in one breath: every training app prescribes workouts without knowing where you are standing; every route app draws static lines without knowing what your body needs today. Waypoint answers the question both halves ignore: "where should I run, right now, from here, for me." Constraint-based route generation is the hero feature; an adaptive coaching layer connects your training state to where you actually go; voice navigation executes it; privacy is day-one architecture.

Gate decisions

Four calls are yours to make before the concept locks and product work begins. Recommended options are highlighted in green with the reasoning beside them.

1 · Lock the concept?

Heaviest decision

The concept: route generation as the hero (free tier anchors here), adaptive coaching as the paid layer, voice navigation as the execution surface, privacy zones built in from day one. Explicitly NOT: a social network, a route library, multi-sport, a plan-quality brand war with Runna.

Lock as writtenRecommended. Every element traces to evidence; amendments after interviews stay possible via decision records.
Lock with amendmentsTell me what to change and why.
HoldDelays Phase 5; the Strava clock keeps running.

2 · Positioning statement

A · Route-firstRecommended. "For committed amateur runners who never quite know where today's run should go, Waypoint is the running app that generates the right route for you, right now, from wherever you stand." Claims the entire empty quadrant; safety and coaching survive inside it as messages.
B · Safety-firstStrongest single evidence base, but narrows the audience and carries claim-liability risk if it leads the brand.
C · Coach-firstPrices you against free Garmin and Apple, brands you against Runna. Weakest option per the competitive map.

3 · Tagline direction

"Your AI Running Coach" claims the exact category that Garmin and Apple are commoditizing and that Runna owns. The research recommends evolving it.

"Know where to run"Recommended. Shortest, ownable, route-led, and it doubles as the product promise.
"The app that knows where you should run"More explicit, less punchy.
"Never wonder where to run"Pain-led framing.
"The coach that knows the way"Bridge option if you want coach continuity.
Keep "Your AI Running Coach"Not recommended, for the category reasons above.

4 · The H1 reframe

Your founding insight was travel ("where do I run in an unfamiliar place"). The evidence says: the travel pain is real and constant, but runners usually solve it with 15 to 20 minute workarounds. Two needs showed stronger daily-frequency evidence: safety-aware routing and route boredom at home.

Accept the reframeRecommended. Travel becomes the activation moment and demo story; safety plus home novelty carry daily retention. Same product, same engine, sharper story.
Keep travel as headlinePreserves the founding story but leads with the weaker retention evidence.
Defer to interviewsSafe but slows messaging work; interviews can still recalibrate later.

Deferred by design: whether safety-critical routing must live in the free tier (an ethics and pricing call) lands in Phase 6 with the pricing work. The real-user interview backlog runs alongside Phase 5 and can adjust calibration without unlocking the concept.

What matters most

  • Participation is booming, led by Gen Z: global runners grew from 672M to 785M (2022 to 2025); record London Marathon ballot (1.13M applications); Strava run clubs quadrupled.
  • The enabling stack is nearly free: OpenStreetMap routing, free elevation data, WeatherKit's free tier, Apple's on-device AI. The moat must be personalization and proprietary context data, not routing algorithms.
  • 2025 consolidated the map: Strava bought Runna; Bending Spoons bought Komoot and cut ~85 percent of staff. The two assets that could fuse training and routing landed with different owners.
  • Platform squeeze: Garmin Connect+ ($6.99/mo) and Apple Workout Buddy commoditize generic AI coaching chat. Differentiation must be what they do not do.

Compliance guardrails (day-one architecture)

  • Location is the most sensitive surface: routes anchored at home reveal home addresses (the Strava heatmap precedent). Privacy zones and private-by-default are mandatory from the MVP.
  • Heart rate and training load are GDPR Article 9 special category data; the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and Washington MHMD apply to consumer fitness apps.
  • EU AI Act Article 50 (disclose users are talking to AI) applies from August 2, 2026.
  • A 15-item MVP compliance checklist is ready in regulatory-compliance.md.

The threat that matters

Strava connecting Runna's plan engine to its route generation is the kill shot. Estimated 50 to 60 percent likelihood of a v1 within 18 months, but only 20 to 30 percent for full constraint depth (weather, crossings, safety). Early-warning signals now monitored: Runna beta strings about routes, Strava routing-engineer job postings, bundle marketing language shifts.

Data enclosure is the multiplier: Strava's API bans AI/ML use of its data and Garmin is paused, so personalization must be HealthKit-first and the moat must be data Waypoint builds itself: street-crossing graphs, lighting and safety scoring, micro-weather.

Never compete on

Community and the social graph (post TO Strava) · trail content libraries · coaching brand wars · hardware · price-zero basics · multi-sport breadth.

The four personas

Marcus
The committed amateur racer · PRIMARY TARGET

Runs four times a week around a job and family, races a spring half and a fall marathon, coaches himself with apps, and is bored to death of his own three loops. The only segment with proven willingness to pay.

Priya
The traveling professional · ACTIVATION MOMENT

A consultant in a different city every week, mid marathon block, standing in a hotel lobby at 6 a.m. with 10 miles to do and no idea where to run them. The sharpest demo of the hero feature.

Elena
The safety-first city runner · STRONGEST EVIDENCE

Plans every run around daylight, lighting, and escape routes before pace. For her the route IS the safety decision. 54 percent of surveyed women runners changed routes over safety; zero products accept safety as an input.

Jake
The ambitious beginner · SECONDARY TARGET

Nine months in via a run club, signed up for a first half marathon, motivated, overconfident, and one aggressive training plan away from a shin splint. The Gen Z growth pipeline.

The main job Waypoint is hired for

"When it is time to run and my usual route does not fit today (a new city, a dark morning, a heat wave, a tempo workout, or plain boredom with my loops), hand me the right route for me, right now, from here, so I can spend my energy running instead of planning and worrying."

Phase 4 · Synthesis

Four documents: opportunity, positioning, hypothesis review, and the concept proposed for lock. Full detail in research/04-synthesis/.

The opportunity in three sentences

Every training app prescribes workouts without knowing where the runner is standing, and every route app plans static lines without knowing what the runner's body needs today. Waypoint claims that empty intersection with constraint-based adaptive route generation as the acquisition wedge, activated by the travel moment and retained daily by safety-aware routing and route novelty, with the coaching layer as the paid tier and the defensible seam. The enabling stack costs near zero at the margin, the one open personalization pathway (HealthKit) matches the iOS-first plan, and the window before the only credible incumbent assembles the same capability is estimated at 12 to 18 months.

The feature architecture

Free tier anchor

On-demand route generation with constraints: distance, elevation, surface, crossing minimization, safety-aware and weather-aware routing. Route novelty ("roads you have not run"). Travel mode.

Paid adaptive layer

Training-state-aware generation (today's workout shapes the route). Adaptive routines that survive real life, calibrated between Runna-too-hard and TrainAsONE-too-soft. A coach that explains its reasoning.

Execution surface

Voice-guided turn-by-turn so the phone stays pocketed. Apple Watch companion. Post-run share to Strava (never compete with the social graph).

The honest risk ledger (what an investor must believe)

  • H5 is the weak link: the wedge alone is a $5M to $30M ARR business. The venture story requires the expansion path: iOS English-first, then Android, more geographies, then the same route engine pointed at walking, cycling, and hiking.
  • The Strava clock is real: speed is a business requirement, not a preference.
  • The safety tightrope: safety-aware routing is the strongest evidence AND the biggest liability. Never market "safe routes" as a guarantee.
  • Validation debt: everything so far is desk research. The interview backlog runs alongside Phase 5.
